01 / Architecture

Three parts.
One record.

Each part does a different job. The room keeps the local record; Theo supplies framework knowledge and method options; the intelligence layer reads that context and suggests a useful next move. The person remains responsible for promotion and decisions.

01 / MindrianOS

The room holds the work

Filed notes, claims, questions, decisions, and their relationships remain available after the conversation ends.

02 / Theo

The method gives direction

Frameworks and method knowledge help frame the next question and possible sequence of work. The room keeps what people actually file.

03 / Intelligence layer

The advisor reads the room

It can surface tensions and suggest next steps using the room and available methods. Suggestions stay advisory.

02 / A piece of work

A claim has a path.

Follow an interview note through the system. The first four moves describe current behavior. The fifth shows the next user experience B1 and B2 are meant to support.

01 / BUILT NOW

File the material

An interview note or unpublished document joins the local room alongside methods and prior work.

02 / BUILT NOW

Make a claim

A claim lands as proposed. Its status does not change because an answer sounds persuasive.

03 / BUILT NOW

Expose tension

The room can surface contradiction with an earlier filed claim as a typed relation.

04 / BUILT NOW

Human review

Claim confirmation is a separate human action. The room can also list unanswered questions and claims without support.

05 / NEXT EXPERIENCE

Show the checking trail

B1 and B2 aim to make verification and changes in the governing question inspectable in the normal workflow.

Critical distinction: Human confirmation says a person accepted a claim. It does not, by itself, prove that the claim was checked against an external source.

05 / The next capability

Make the invisible checks visible.

The ambition is specific: let a future reviewer reconstruct what was checked and notice when the question organizing the work changed. These diagrams describe the target interaction, with existing code pieces called out below.

B1 / partial substrate

What was this claim checked against?

A verification event should retain the target, method, result, actor, and time. A room portrait can then count the status of claims without turning the count into a truth score.

Example data is illustrative. No percentages or sample counts are presented as product measurements.

B2 / partial substrate

Why did the question move?

A changed governing thought should be legible as a change of frame. If a person explains what the old question missed, that can support a refinement. An unexplained move should remain visible as a relocation or unresolved change.

FRAME 1 → changed question → FRAME 2
origin + predecessor + explanation
pause before the new answer
  • In code: frame metadata, version handles, and change classification helper.
  • Still needed: a user-facing history and pre-answer gate across answer paths.

Frame change is not evidence that the new frame is better; the person supplies that judgment.

The acceptance test for the future slide: an officer can reopen a room and inspect a claim’s checking trail and the frame history that shaped an answer, through the normal product flow. Until then, present B1 and B2 as work in progress.
