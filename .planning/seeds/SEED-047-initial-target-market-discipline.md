# SEED-047 - Initial-target-market discipline (5 buyers, not a $47B TAM)

**Registered:** 2026-07-01 (navigator-directed; tester finding N2)
**Class:** CODE (methodology refinement) | **Status:** seed
**Grounding:** docs/testers/oliver-kuntz/FEEDBACK.md (2026-07-01); docs/testers/FINDINGS-2026-07-01.md N2. Frameworks: Peter Thiel (Zero to One), John Mullins (New Business Road Test).

## The finding

Mindry surfaced a $47B TAM for a specific technology. Lawrence: "target market is not $47 billion. Give me the names of 5 people who would die to buy this. Think small AND big." Peter Thiel: the single most important entrepreneurial decision is the choice of INITIAL target market; "show me the 100 people who will live or die because of this." Mullins: think small and big at the same time; everybody thinks big, so push them to think small.

## Why it matters

A giant TAM number is a red flag, not a signal. The valuable output is the INITIAL beachhead: who are the ideal first customers, named, and what is the realistic upside if it goes perfectly. The methodology surfaces (validate, mullins, value-proposition) should push initial-target-market + "5 named buyers" BEFORE any TAM/SAM/SOM, and treat a bare giant TAM as a gap to challenge.

## Proposed scope

- Refine /mos:mullins + /mos:validate + /mos:value-proposition prompts: lead with initial-target-market ("name 5 who would die to buy this"), think-small-and-big, and flag a bare TAM as insufficient.
- Optional: a "beachhead" check in the grading / triple-validation compass.
