<objective>
Close the two candidates the primary research did not cover, because it was scoped to the five
named PWS engines rather than the other 108 commands. Scout A found `explore-opportunity`;
Scout C found `trending-to-absurd`. Both are cases where a command's dispatch SHAPE is
undocumented, so every future reader re-derives it or guesses.

Purpose: forking is now the platform default, so "what shape does this dispatch in" stopped
being an implementation detail and became a documented contract.
Output: two corrected command files, one tripwire, and one navigator decision.

**Planner risk flag, surfaced deliberately rather than guessed. Read before Task 2.**
The planning brief anticipated converting explore-opportunity's four legs to a parallel
Agent-tool dispatch by editing "commands/explore-opportunity.md's own dispatch instructions."
A direct read shows there ARE none: the file is 122 lines with zero occurrences of Agent, Task,
subagent, dispatch, parallel, or sequential, and it delegates entirely to
`lib/core/eureka/explore-chain.cjs::exploreOpportunity`, which calls the shared
`lib/core/chain-executor.cjs::runChain`. Three further facts make a blind conversion unsafe:
1. `runChain` couples the legs through CONTROL flow even though no leg consumes another's
   analysis: a `quality_early_stop` branch ends the chain when a step returns LOW quality, and
   `gateFn` halts on posture. Parallel dispatch removes both.
2. That early stop is a cost control, not a formality. This command's own `hitl_why` commits
   that "Exploration spends navigator-controlled research cost", and two of the four legs are
   web legs riding the frozen deep_research reach. Firing all four in parallel spends all four
   even when the deep_research leg returns `insufficient_evidence`.
3. `lib/core/chain-executor.cjs` is under an explicit zero-diff arm in `tests/run-all-264.sh`
   pinned to base SHA `c7c33eea449f6f227c4cfbb86f220acaac9b5ab8`. The shared executor is
   protected by a shipped gate, not merely by convention.
Therefore this plan documents the coupling and puts the build-or-not question to the navigator
at a checkpoint. It does not modify the shared executor and does not guess.
</objective>
