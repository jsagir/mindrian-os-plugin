<objective>
Mint a SEPARATE render-coverage registry keyed on the .cjs render entry points (C-1) -- a
distinct keyspace, NOT a column on the markdown connector ledger.

Per the navigator's CORRECTED two-plane framing (docs/CANON-PART-11-RENDER-TWIN-PROPOSAL.md
"Design consequence (separate render registry - CORRECTED)"): the two planes share a DISCIPLINE
(born-wired, hard-FAIL, registry-driven) but NOT a keyspace. Plane 1 is INVOCATION (does Larry
reach the right thing -- CIRS R2/R9, born-wired in data/connector-coverage-ledger.json, keyed on
MARKDOWN surfaces commands/skills/agents). Plane 2 is RENDER (does the reached gate FIRE its card
-- R15, this phase). The render entry points are `.cjs` call sites in lib/ and scripts/ (the 14
pickShape callers + the 2 renderDial callers), and most have NO owning markdown surface, so
co-residency on the connector ledger is undefined for them. The corrected design is a DEDICATED
render-coverage registry (data/render-coverage-registry.json) keyed on those `.cjs` entry points,
walked by exhaustive AST/grep enumeration. check-render-coverage.cjs (178-02) reads THIS registry.

REJECTED ALTERNATIVE (recorded per D-178-07): a "one ledger, two columns" design adding a
render_coverage column to data/connector-coverage-ledger.json. Rejected after the plan-checker
verified it against the live tree: the connector ledger is keyed on markdown surfaces, but render
entry points are `.cjs` call sites, 10 of 16 with NO owning markdown surface. Co-residency is
undefined for a distinct keyspace; forcing it would mis-key render onto markdown surfaces that do
not own those call sites. The render plane gets its own registry.

R-3 (registry-completeness drift) is the new single point of failure: born-wired only holds if
EVERY render entry point is in the registry. The cure is NOT co-residency -- it is the
EXHAUSTIVENESS FLOOR test (Task 2): a render entry point that exists in the code but is ABSENT
from the registry FAILS the build. That exhaustiveness check is the structural guarantee
co-residency was meant to provide. The Phase 177 slip (dial-selector.cjs F.7-dial minted with no
explicit registry entry) is exactly what the floor catches: a new render surface cannot escape.

Per D-178-02 this REUSES the existing CIRS generator pattern (build-connector-registry.cjs is the
template for the walk + the --check byte-compare; Part 7), it does not duplicate it. Per D-178-04
additive only: this mints a NEW local artifact; it touches NO existing ledger, NO reach/posture/
edge/node, NO Part 3 frozen contract (MAX_K=3, DIAL_REACH_K=6, the 0.70/0.15 gate, the 6-reach
bank, the glyphs), and opens NO Brain wire (Part 8).

NOTE on the caller count (LOW reconciliation): the live tree has exactly 14 files calling
pickShape( -- 13 external callers PLUS lib/hmi/selector-dispatcher.cjs itself (pickShape is
defined and invoked there; the dispatcher self-call counts as a render entry point because it is a
real call site reached at runtime) -- and 2 files calling renderDial( (lib/hmi/dial-presenter.cjs,
scripts/intent-classifier.cjs). 14 + 2 = 16 render entry points, matching the proposal doc's
"~16 .cjs sites." The walk MUST enumerate all 16, the dispatcher self-call included.

Purpose: give the render plane its own born-wired registry so a future render surface CANNOT
escape (the exhaustiveness floor) and the connector ledger stays byte-untouched (separate
keyspace; R-3 dissolved by the floor, not by co-residency).
Output: scripts/build-render-coverage.cjs + data/render-coverage-registry.json, a green
tests/test-render-registry-exhaustive.cjs, and the tests/run-all-178.sh aggregator scaffold.
</objective>
