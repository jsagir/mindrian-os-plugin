## Goal

`/mos:persona` works with the six hats and with research. The six hat personas are produced from the room. Each hat makes its own research. Each hat then does its persona work from its own perspective, in isolation. Larry merges the six readings and the navigator rules on each tension.

## Scope

S1 Produce: six hat personas built from the room by six fresh persona-analyst agents. Fixed part: the hat discipline. Room part: focus, hat questions, generic research handles.
S2 Research: each researching hat runs its own research through the existing Bono leg (lib/core/bono/cell-fanout.cjs, lib/core/bono/persona-research.cjs). Red and Blue make no web call.
S3 Persona work: each hat analyses the target with its own research only. A hat cannot cite another hat's sources.
S4 Synthesis: Larry, with the existing tension map (lib/core/synthesizers/tension-map.cjs). A hat that failed is "not run", never "no objection".
S5 Two modes, each with its own identity: deep (S1 to S4) and quick (six hats in one pass, no research, named as a fast look).
S6 Gates: brief confirm; persona accept; one approval card per researching hat with its exact search strings; tension ruling.
S7 Lifetime: personas are reused until the room changes.

## Model: elephant-goldfish (EGM)

Owner: EGM is a good model of persona work. Source: `~/.claude/plugins/marketplaces/egm` (read 2026-11-25 by a read-only agent; no code was run).

Rules that carry over:
- Isolation: no hat reads another hat before the synthesis.
- Fresh context per persona. A same-context run is a different thing and must say so.
- Withheld context: a hat does not see the conversation or Larry's view of the target. It gets an explicit brief.
- A persona is its stance, what it checks, and what it must NOT judge.
- A hat that failed is "not run", never "no objection".
- A settled tension is not re-opened by a later run.

Rules that do NOT carry over:
- Zero context. A hat needs the room; withhold the conversation and the other hats, not the room.
- Fixed persona files. Here the personas are produced from the room (owner ruling).
- Loop until clean. Six hats never reach zero tension; the disagreement is the product.
- Severity ranks and auto-fix. Only the navigator confirms a venture claim (Part 9).
- Dedup that merges findings. Two hats that agree are a convergence signal and stay attributed.

## Owner adjustments 2026-11-25 (cycle v2)

Source: web research of the same day on other Six Hats agent systems (search excerpts, not full pages): Castro's Six Hats Deliberation Harness (leonardocastro.dev), xverges/sixhats, NeuralHats, the ysskrishna and juanallo skills, PTFA (arXiv 2503.12499), Rachmann's Six Thinking Chatbots, Liang et al. on multi-agent debate (arXiv 2305.19118). None of them builds hat personas from context with own research per hat in isolation.

### The cycle, ten steps (replaces the four-step order above where they differ)

1. Triage: does the question need deep mode, or is quick mode enough.
2. Brief: target, governing question, reading list; the navigator confirms.
3. Produce: six hat personas from the room, each in a fresh context.
4. Shared facts: White researches first; its sourced evidence package goes to every hat. This changes the earlier design, where White's research was private. Owner answer: "ok" to the adjusted cycle; the shared White package is DEFAULT CHOSEN and the owner may overrule.
5. Private research: Black, Yellow and Green each research their own side; one approval card per researching hat. Red and Blue never search.
6. Independent readings: each hat reads the target alone.
7. Check: a hat cites only White's shared package and its own sources; a failed hat is "not run".
8. Merge: Larry, under three rules. Dissent of every hat is recorded first. The merge goes only through the existing tension map and every finding stays attributed. The merge adds no new fact, risk, idea or recommendation.
9. The navigator rules on each tension.
10. Measure: a way to run deep and quick on the same question and compare what each found. No source shows that more agents is better by default.

### Per-hat rules

- White: goes first, shares its evidence, lists missing data as well as found data.
- Red: no research, no justification, raw reaction only.
- Black: disconfirming evidence first; its reading is never averaged with others.
- Yellow: cites real success cases, or says none was found.
- Green: generates only, never evaluates; its research imports ideas from other fields.
- Blue: reads the process only (what was skipped, which hat was thin, what to ask next); adds no content.

### Jobs: when to use the bundle (more than one)

Owner: the bundle has more than one job. /mos:persona carries serves_jtbd ["prepare-pitch"] today. Second draft of 2026-11-25 (the first draft named the tool in every "want" slot, so each statement described the solution and not the situation). No statement names the hats or the command. All pass slotErrors, the 40-word limit and the no-digit rule of Phase 400. All provisional until the owner rules. The job id before each statement is the nearest id of data/job-vocabulary.json.

- validate-idea: When I am about to build on a claim that only I have examined, I want to have it checked from several independent sides with fresh evidence, so I can find where it breaks before it costs me.
- explore: When I research a question and keep finding what I already expected, I want to send separate lines of inquiry at it from opposing stances, so I can see evidence I would never have searched for.
- decide-pursue: When I must commit to an opportunity or drop it and feeling and fact are tangled, I want to pull apart the facts, the upside, the risks and my gut reaction, so I can see which one is driving me.
- surface-contradiction: When everything in my room seems to agree, I want to have it read by perspectives that did not write it, so I can find the tensions I have stopped noticing.
- compare-options: When each option in front of me has someone arguing for it, I want to judge every option on the same sides, so I can compare them without the loudest voice winning.
- prepare-pitch: When I am about to face an investor, a partner or a customer, I want to hear the hardest questions from each kind of listener before they ask them, so I can answer in the room and not afterwards.
- find-problem: When my hypothesis is still fuzzy and I am about to put numbers on it, I want to look at it from each side in turn, so I can sharpen it into a hypothesis worth the analysis. (Close to Theo's second ruled Six Thinking Hats statement in data/framework-jobs.json.)
- navigate: When my team argues in circles because each person defends a position, I want to make everyone look at one side at a time, so I can move the discussion forward. (Close to Theo's main ruled Six Thinking Hats statement.)
- audit-room: When new evidence arrives that could change my case, I want to see how it lands from each side, so I can update what I believe without overreacting.
