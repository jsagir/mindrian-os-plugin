## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Hybrid (Structured Extraction -> Content Generation pipeline, batch-orchestrated)

**Description:**
A batch pipeline that turns each student's diarized 5-minute pitch transcript into one written formative feedback artifact, for 200+ undergraduates in Amnon Dekel's HUJI entrepreneurship/digital course. Per submission: a cheap extraction pass produces a quote-anchored evidence JSON, then the Mindrian methodology chain (deep-grade -> mullins -> build-thesis scored -> structure-argument) generates feedback delivered as a Minto pyramid (governing thought first, 2-3 branches at the student's level). Students receive the feedback; the instructor is the real customer and judges the feedback quality itself against "better than a TA." Good = every claim grounded in what the student actually said (quote/timestamp), tiered to course level not investor level, each branch ending in a concrete teachable next step, consistent tone and severity across the whole cohort, and language-gentle toward non-native English speakers working from machine transcripts.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. **Fabricated attribution.** Feedback praises, criticizes, or quotes something the transcript does not contain, or flags a "missing" element the student explicitly covered. One hallucinated claim in front of the instructor destroys trust in all 200 artifacts.
2. **Cohort inconsistency.** Two comparable pitches receive different severity, depth, or scores because of model drift, prompt drift, or cache state mid-batch. Fairness at N=200 is the instructor's make-or-break criterion.
3. **Investor-tier harshness.** The full ten-questions/Mullins gauntlet applied un-tiered to a course assignment - "half the class fails question 2 and learns nothing" (navigator ruling). The validation battery must be tiered by the course rubric.
4. **Generic templating / double-punished metacognition.** Feedback a student could not recognize as being about THEIR pitch, or feedback that lists a gap the student already self-identified as if it were a discovered deficiency (sample 2 names its own gaps - reward that, never punish it twice).
5. **Student content egress.** Pitch transcript content crossing to Brain or leaving via any network tool. Breaks Canon Part 8 and the local-processing privacy story being sold to the university.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** Higher education - formative assessment in entrepreneurship pedagogy (venture-creation course, intro level)

**User Population:** 200+ undergraduates at Hebrew University of Jerusalem, many non-native English speakers, each submitting a ~2-minute spoken pitch as a diarized machine transcript. The students READ the feedback; the course instructor (Amnon Dekel) is the evaluating customer - he judges whether the feedback beats a TA, and his trust decides the semester license.

**Stakes Level:** Medium (individual feedback - formative, no grade attached in the pilot). Rises to High at the cohort level: consistency across 200 students and the instructor's trust are the make-or-break, and this is MindrianOS's first paying job.

**Output Consequence:** A student reads one written feedback artifact and (ideally) revises their venture thinking before the next milestone. The instructor reads a sample of artifacts and decides whether Mindrian replaces TA feedback at semester scale. No grade rides on the pilot output, so a bad artifact costs learning and reputation, not academic standing - but a fabricated or unfair artifact costs the entire deal.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

```
Dimension: Grounding in the student's own words
Good: Every substantive claim in the feedback points at something the student actually said - a quote or timestamp. E.g., flags SafeScan's "smart light sensor" as asserted-not-evidenced by citing the student's own line (1:10 region); credits the study-app pitch for naming risks AND mitigations with the quoted passage.
Bad: Praises or criticizes content absent from the transcript; a fabricated critique; feedback that could be pasted onto another student's pitch verbatim without anyone noticing.
Stakes: Critical
Source: LLM-feedback faithfulness research - fabricated critiques and ungrounded suggestions are the documented critical limitation of AI writing feedback; also the anti-hallucination anchor already designed into EvidenceSchema (verbatim quotes).
```

```
Dimension: Feed-up / feed-back / feed-forward completeness (actionability)
Good: Each pyramid branch names the criterion (feed-up), what this pitch did against it with evidence (feed-back), and ONE concrete next step the student can do before the next milestone (feed-forward) - e.g., "run 5 interviews with allergic diners to test the 'afraid to trust the waiter' claim." Length proportionate to a 2-minute pitch.
Bad: Verdict without a path ("market analysis is weak"); next steps above course level (term sheets, CAC/LTV modeling); a 4,000-word essay; feedback that only summarizes what the student said.
Stakes: High
Source: Hattie & Timperley feedback model (feed-up/feed-back/feed-forward) - the standard framework education practitioners use to judge written formative feedback; confirmed as the grounding frame in current LLM-feedback quality research.
```

```
Dimension: Course-tier calibration (student, not founder)
Good: Validation questions tiered to what the assignment actually teaches - the samples reveal the implicit skeleton: problem -> value -> prototype -> risks+mitigation -> critical path -> team -> gaps. Evidence-vs-assertion awareness rewarded at intro depth; tone assumes a first venture.
Bad: The full investor gauntlet - failing a pitch on unit economics, moat, or defensibility the assignment never asked for; grade-and-compliment theater (Canon Part 12); praise inflation that tells a weak pitch it is strong.
Stakes: High
Source: Navigator calibration ruling (15.7.2026) + entrepreneurship-pedagogy practice: course pitch rubrics use behavior-based levels as formative tools, deliberately distinct from investor screening criteria. Which ten-questions at what depth is the HUJI calibration workshop's output.
```

```
Dimension: Metacognition rewarded, never double-punished
Good: When a student names their own gap (sample 2: "what is still missing... deeper market research, competitor analysis, user testing"), the feedback explicitly credits the self-assessment and deepens it - HOW to do the competitor analysis, not THAT it is missing.
Bad: Listing the student's self-identified gap back as a discovered deficiency, scoring it as if the student were unaware.
Stakes: High
Source: The customer's own sample data (sample-2) + self-assessment literature in entrepreneurship education: having students assess their own work builds metacognition, and instructors treat honest gap-naming as a skill to reinforce.
```

```
Dimension: Cohort fairness and language gentleness
Good: Same rubric, same pinned model, same depth for all 200; comparable pitches earn comparable scores and tone; disfluencies ("vali- validating", "uh"), diarization noise ("Recording in progress" as Speaker 1), and non-native phrasing ("Restaurant kitchen cannot guarantee") are NEVER treated as content weaknesses.
Bad: Severity drift between student 1 and student 200; feedback that reads as language correction; two near-identical pitches with visibly different verdicts.
Stakes: Critical
Source: LLM grading-consistency research (inconsistent structure and severity across runs is a documented failure) + baseline fairness expectations of academic assessment; operationalized by the pinned-model-ID and frozen-prefix rules in Sections 3-4.
```

```

```

```
Good: Every pws_pitch_feedback invocation - whether inside the 200-student batch or an ad hoc MCP call - runs through the SAME pinned model, frozen rubric prefix, and G1-G6 guardrails (grounding gate, schema gate, cost fuse, length/tone bound) the batch pipeline enforces, so a TA reading two artifacts side by side (one from the batch, one from an ad hoc call) cannot tell them apart in rigor or severity.
Bad: An ad hoc MCP call reaches a student or instructor without passing the human sampling pass (Jonathan's pre-delivery review, Section 7 of this AI-SPEC) that the HUJI-signed-off pipeline treats as part of the delivery contract - the "100% of first 10 units" shakedown, the anchor-probe drift check, and the metacognition-credit review all live in the BATCH orchestrator, not in the tool itself, so a standalone call is graded by the same engine but delivered without the same quality floor.
Stakes: High
Source: Hattie & Timperley practitioner frame (already the grounding frame for D7 in this AI-SPEC) plus this phase's own Section 7 smart-sampling design - the instructor's "better than a TA" trust bar was calibrated against a pipeline that includes human review before delivery; a delivery path that skips that review is a different, unvalidated product even though it shares the grading engine.
```

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **Fabricated critique / hallucinated error.** The best-documented failure of LLM student feedback: fluent, plausible comments identifying "errors" that do not exist in the student's work, or content not grounded in it. Here it manifests as feedback quoting or paraphrasing things the pitch never said, or marking covered elements (sample 2's risks section) as missing. Mitigation is structural: the quote-anchored evidence JSON is the only thing the spine grades.
2. **Bland templating and overpraising.** Untuned LLMs produce generic, over-positive feedback; research names "hallucination and overpraising" as the typical pair. At N=200 of one assignment type, template convergence is the default outcome, and the instructor - who reads many artifacts side by side - detects interchangeability faster than any single student.
3. **Severity drift across the cohort.** A failure mode unique to batch automation: model alias retargeting, prompt/rubric edits, or cache state changing mid-batch means early and late students are graded under different conditions. A TA drifts too, but an automated system sold as "consistent" is judged harshly for it. (Pitfall 1 in Section 3 exists because of this.)
4. **Transcription-artifact punishment.** The input is a machine transcript of non-native spoken English: disfluencies, self-corrections, diarization glitches. Feedback that penalizes delivery or language artifacts as if they were content weaknesses is both unfair and, for this population, demotivating - the exact opposite of formative intent.
5. **Scope-drift via general-purpose exposure (new, v1.1 MCP delivery seam).** A tool built and calibrated for one consented, closed pilot becomes reachable by any connected MCP client. The failure is not a code bug - the grounding gates (G1-G6) still fire - it is a governance failure: the tool cannot itself distinguish an invocation that falls inside HUJI's consented pilot scope from one that does not. Canon Part 8's "Brain stays read-only, generic-handle-only" guarantee remains true regardless of caller, but it answers a narrower question (does data leak to Brain) than the one a FERPA-literate practitioner would ask (is every invocation traceable to an authorized, consented use).
6. **Silent rubric misapplication across course/investor context (new, v1.1 MCP delivery seam).** Without an explicit, required rubric/course-context parameter, any transcript run through `pws_pitch_feedback` is graded under HUJI's intro-course calibration by default. Grading an actual investor pitch under intro-course tiering (or a course pitch under investor tiering) is a domain-correctness failure independent of grounding or tone quality - the feedback can pass every grounding and structure gate and still be evaluating the wrong thing.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

- **Israel Privacy Protection Law, Amendment 13 (in force 14 Aug 2025).** Student pitch transcripts are personal data of identifiable students (voice-derived, course-linked). Amendment 13 tightens consent requirements and the PPA's 2025 AI guidance requires informed consent for feeding personal data into AI systems. Practical shape for the pilot: HUJI is the data controller and owns student notice/consent; Mindrian is a processor. Flag to Amnon that consent/notice for AI processing is HUJI's side of the table - do not silently absorb it.
- **Anthropic API transit.** Transcripts transit the Anthropic API in every design (noted in Section 4b); the Message Batches API is additionally NOT zero-data-retention eligible - one more reason the pilot stays on the headless CLI path. Keep the data-flow statement honest in anything shown to HUJI.
- **Canon Part 8 as the compliance feature.** Student content never egresses to Brain; scoring is local; only generic rubric/calibration handles cross the wire. The canon explicitly names a FERPA persona - FERPA itself does not apply (US law, Israeli institution), but treating transcripts to that standard is the selling point to a university customer. Sell it as such.
- **Academic integrity / contestability.** No grade attaches in the pilot, so appeal exposure is low. If semester-scale feedback later informs grading, contestability expectations rise (students challenging AI-assessed work is an active research area) - a v2 design question, not a pilot blocker.
- **MCP delivery seam narrows, not widens, the school-official exception's safety margin (new, v1.1, 2026-07-16).** FERPA's "school official" exception - the standard legal basis for a vendor processing student data without per-student consent - requires the vendor be under the "direct control" of the institution and use the data "only for the purposes for which it was disclosed." The existing Part-8 selling point ("scoring stays local, only generic handles cross the wire") answers the DATA-EGRESS half of that exception, not the DIRECT-CONTROL / PURPOSE-LIMITATION half. That half depends on Mindrian only using HUJI's transcripts for the service HUJI contracted (this pilot, this rubric, this cohort). A standalone `pws_pitch_feedback` tool reachable by any connected MCP client is, by itself, deployment-scope-agnostic: the LOCAL-vs-BRAIN egress guarantee holds regardless of caller, but "was this invocation inside HUJI's consented scope" becomes a governance question the tool does not currently answer, and "nobody but Jonathan currently has the MCP server connected" is an informal boundary, not an enforced one. Israel's Amendment 13 consent requirement (above) has the identical shape - HUJI's consent covers a specific processing purpose, not an open-ended callable capability. **Recommendation:** require the rubric/course-context parameter (see rubric ingredient above) at the schema level so every invocation is provably scoped to an authorized use, rather than relying on caller trust.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** No external framework - plugin-native composition (runChain/composeWorkflow chain per submission + new CJS/bash batch orchestrator driving headless `claude -p` sessions, one per submission)

**Version:** Claude Code CLI >= 2.1.205 (verified local: 2.1.210 - `--json-schema` requires >= 2.1.205); Node >= 22.5.0; zod ^3.25.76 (in-stack); plugin at current dev HEAD. Batch model IDs pinned in batch.config.json at batch start (see Section 4).

**Rationale:**
The per-submission pipeline (deep-grade -> mullins -> build-thesis scored -> structure-argument) already runs on the runChain spine; all four commands are autonomous_safe and resolve via composeWorkflow today. The only genuine build is the N=200 outer loop, and a CJS/bash orchestrator spawning headless claude -p sessions gives per-submission process isolation, filesystem-checkpoint resume, and keeps cost routing inside model-profiles.cjs where the $4-5/unit ceiling is enforced. Any external framework would mint a second orchestration brain next to runChain (Canon Part 7 violation) and buys nothing for a fixed linear 4-step chain (graph-framework-for-linear-flow anti-pattern).

**Alternatives Considered:**
- Claude Agent SDK (@anthropic-ai/claude-agent-sdk) - fallback confined to the batch orchestrator seam only, if headless CLI proves brittle at N=200 (session lifecycle, structured result capture, retry). ESM/TS friction accepted only at that seam.
- LangChain.js / LangGraph.js - ELIMINATED: graph framework for a fixed linear 4-step chain (anti-pattern), bypasses model-profiles.cjs cost routing, mints a second orchestration brain next to runChain (Canon Part 7 violation).
- Python frameworks (CrewAI, AutoGen, LlamaIndex-py, etc.) - ELIMINATED: no-Python-on-core-path constraint.

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| Claude Agent SDK | Not eliminated - held in reserve as the batch-orchestrator-seam fallback only, not a replacement for the CLI spine |
| LangChain.js / LangGraph.js | Graph framework for a fixed linear 4-step chain; bypasses `model-profiles.cjs` cost routing; mints a second orchestration brain next to `runChain` (Canon Part 7 violation) |
| Python frameworks (CrewAI, AutoGen, LlamaIndex-py) | No-Python-on-core-path constraint (CJS-only stack) |
| Supabase + GitHub Actions (proposed this session, 2026-07-16) | Two new hosted services against a named "no server infrastructure" constraint; duplicates `huji-batch.cjs`'s existing checkpoint ledger and `rubric-huji.md`'s git-based versioning; the `git commit` step in the proposed CI workflow would have broken the T-229-08-05 invariant that student output never gets committed to the repo |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** Partial - Anthropic/Claude Code lock-in is accepted consciously: the whole product IS a Claude Code plugin, so the marginal lock-in of headless claude -p orchestration is zero. The methodology commands, rubric, anchors, and evidence schemas are plain markdown/JSON and portable by construction.

### Entry Point Pattern

Two layers. Inner: one headless `claude -p` session per submission, running the registered `PWS_grading` chain via the plugin's own commands. Outer: a CJS batch orchestrator looping N submissions with checkpoint/resume.

**Recipe name (navigator ruling, 15.7.2026): `PWS_grading`.** The registered command-resolver recipe/pipeline identifier for the deep-grade -> mullins -> build-thesis -> structure-argument chain. All slash-command invocations, `recipe-maps.cjs` registrations, and file names below use this name - not "pitch-feedback" (that string survives only as the phase-directory label and the opportunity-bank filename, both already committed).

```javascript
#!/usr/bin/env node
// scripts/huji-batch.cjs - outer loop (minimal working shape)
'use strict';
const { spawnSync } = require('node:child_process');
const fs = require('node:fs');
const path = require('node:path');

const CONFIG = JSON.parse(fs.readFileSync('batch.config.json', 'utf8'));
// CONFIG = { model: "claude-opus-4-8", pluginDir: "/home/jsagi/dev/MindrianOS-Plugin",
//            budgetPerUnitUsd: 3.0, maxTurns: 40, schemaPath: "schemas/feedback-result.json" }

function runSubmission(subId, transcriptPath, scratchRoom) {
  const prompt = `/mos:pipeline PWS_grading --transcript ${transcriptPath}`; // slash commands
  // expand in -p mode (Claude Code >= 2.1.x, documented): include /command in the prompt string.
  const res = spawnSync('claude', [
    '-p', prompt,
    '--plugin-dir', CONFIG.pluginDir,          // deterministic plugin load, session-only
    '--model', CONFIG.model,                    // FULL pinned ID - never the moving alias
    '--output-format', 'json',                  // gives result, session_id, total_cost_usd
    '--json-schema', fs.readFileSync(CONFIG.schemaPath, 'utf8'), // validated structured_output
    '--permission-mode', 'dontAsk',             // locked-down: only explicit allows run
    '--allowedTools', 'Read,Write,Edit,Bash(node lib/core/*)',
    '--max-turns', String(CONFIG.maxTurns),     // runaway-session fuse
    '--max-budget-usd', String(CONFIG.budgetPerUnitUsd), // per-unit cost fuse (hard stop)
    '--no-session-persistence',                 // 200 runs = no session-file buildup on disk
  ], { cwd: scratchRoom, encoding: 'utf8', timeout: 20 * 60 * 1000 });

  if (res.status !== 0) return { ok: false, err: (res.stderr || '').slice(0, 2000) };
  const out = JSON.parse(res.stdout);           // { result, structured_output, total_cost_usd, ... }
  return { ok: !out.is_error, out };
}
```

Key mechanics confirmed against current docs (July 2026):
- `--output-format json` returns a payload with `result` (text), `structured_output` (when `--json-schema` is passed), `session_id`, `is_error`, and `total_cost_usd` with a per-model cost breakdown - the orchestrator's per-unit cost ledger comes free.
- Plugin loading in headless: without `--bare`, `claude -p` auto-discovers everything in `~/.claude` (non-deterministic across machines). The deterministic form is `--plugin-dir <path>` pointing at the dev repo checkout. `--bare --plugin-dir` is the strictest form but skips OAuth/keychain (needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`); decide at plan time which auth path the pilot uses.
- The `stream-json` `system/init` event carries `plugins` / `plugin_errors` arrays - use it in a preflight run to fail the batch closed if the plugin did not load.

### Common Pitfalls

1. **Model alias drift mid-cohort (fairness bug, not just a config bug).** `--model opus` and the aliases in `MODEL_PROFILES` move when Anthropic ships a new model. If the alias retargets mid-batch, students 1-120 get graded by a different model than students 121-200. Pin the full model ID (e.g. `claude-opus-4-8`) in `batch.config.json` at batch start and pass it explicitly; record it in every per-student result envelope.
2. **`execSync` with single-quote escaping (the existing `scripts/label-topic-forest.cjs:81` pattern) is both blocking and injection-prone.** A transcript containing `'` fragments breaks the quoting; `execSync` also serializes the whole batch. Use `spawn`/`spawnSync` with an args array and no shell, and pipe long content via file paths, not argv (stdin pipes are capped at 10MB since CLI v2.1.128).
3. **Scratch room STATE.md must declare a grading-legal stage.** `model-profiles.cjs` greps STATE.md for `Stage:`; `Pre-Opportunity` maps grading to `null` = skip = hard stop (sweep 1 finding). The scaffold must write `Stage: Validation` (grading resolves to opus at quality tier) or the whole chain dies at step 1 with a confusing "skip".
4. **HITL F.8 gates halt unattended runs.** `deep-grade`/`grade` carry Decision-Gate shapes; in a headless session with `--permission-mode dontAsk` a gate that waits for a human aborts the run. The chain must ride the `autonomous_safe` posture end-to-end (all four target commands are already `autonomous_safe: true` per sweep 4), and the ten-questions 6/10 HALT must become score-and-continue before batch time.
5. **Session-file and background-process residue at N=200.** Default `-p` persists every session to disk (200 x 1 session = clutter, plus resume ambiguity); use `--no-session-persistence`. Background Bash tasks started inside a session are killed ~5s after the final result (v2.1.163+), and background subagents are waited on up to a 10-minute cap (v2.1.182+, `CLAUDE_CODE_PRINT_BG_WAIT_CEILING_MS`) - a stuck subagent stalls one submission for 10 minutes, so keep the chain single-session, no background fan-out.
6. **Rate-limit collisions under parallelism.** Parallel headless sessions share one API key's rate limits. Claude Code emits `system/api_retry` events (stream-json) with `error: rate_limit` and backs off internally, but throughput collapses silently. Cap concurrency at 3-4 sessions and treat repeated `rate_limit` retries as a signal to drop to serial, not a reason to add retries on top (double-retry amplifies the storm).

**MCP delivery seam pitfalls (v1.1 addition, 2026-07-16 - `pws_pitch_feedback` tool on `bin/mindrian-mcp-server.cjs`):**

7. **`spawnSync` inside an MCP tool handler blocks the WHOLE server process, not just one request.** This is the exact CASCADE-06 defect (`lib/core/room-ops-async.cjs`'s own header: "the MCP path MUST use the async entry point so [the] subprocess call does not block the handler loop") at a much longer wall time. Every other MCP tool call - `room_state`, `room_content`, everything - stalls behind an 8-12 min synchronous subprocess if `runOne`'s two `spawnSync` calls (Stage A up to 5 min, Stage B up to 20 min) are required directly from `lib/mcp/*`. `huji-run-one-async.cjs` (execFile-promisified, Section 4) is the required door; `scripts/huji-run-one.cjs`'s sync `runOne` stays exactly as shipped for `scripts/huji-batch.cjs`.
8. **`execFile` REJECTS the promise on a non-zero exit code; `spawnSync` RETURNS a `.status` field.** A naive `await execFileAsync(...)` swallowing `runOne`'s `if (res.status !== 0) return {...}` checks into a `try/catch` that does not reconstruct `{status, stdout, stderr, error}` silently loses the `stageA_nonzero`/`stageB_nonzero` reason codes the batch's `failures.md` and `--dry-run` assertions depend on. See Section 4's `runClaudeAsync` wrapper - it is the one place this shape gets translated.
9. **The MCP SDK's default per-request timeout is 60 seconds (`DEFAULT_REQUEST_TIMEOUT_MSEC`, verified in the installed `@modelcontextprotocol/sdk@1.29.0` source, `dist/cjs/shared/protocol.js`), and it is a CLIENT-side setting the tool author cannot force.** An 8-12 min `pws_pitch_feedback` call will be aborted by any client that does not raise its own `timeout`/`maxTotalTimeout` or set `resetTimeoutOnProgress: true` on the request. Progress notifications only help if the CALLING client opts into `resetTimeoutOnProgress`; the server has no way to compel that. This is the concrete, protocol-level reason the fire-and-poll fallback (Section 3/4) is not a nice-to-have - it is the only shape that is safe regardless of client timeout configuration, because the tool call itself returns in seconds.
10. **Progress notifications are opt-in and MUST be a silent no-op, never a throw, when unsupported.** `extra._meta.progressToken` (`RequestMetaSchema`) is `undefined` unless the calling client explicitly requested progress on THIS call. `extra.sendNotification(...)` is only valid while the request is still in flight; calling it from code that outlives the handler (e.g. a detached `.then()` after early return) throws. Guard every emit with a `progressToken` presence check and wrap in `try/catch`; a progress-emission failure must never fail the tool's actual result.
11. **`registerTool()`'s `inputSchema`/`outputSchema` accept a Zod schema (v3 or v4, auto-detected) or a Zod raw shape - never a raw JSON Schema object.** `getZodSchemaObject()` (verified in `dist/cjs/server/mcp.js`) explicitly throws `'inputSchema must be a Zod schema or raw shape, received an unrecognized object'` for anything else. `pitch-feedback-schemas.cjs`'s `toJsonSchemas()`/`inlineSchemaJson()` helpers exist ONLY for the `claude` CLI's `--json-schema` argument (DI-1/DI-2 there, a completely different consumer); the MCP wiring passes `EvidenceSchema`/`FeedbackResultSchema` themselves (the zod/v4 objects), never their JSON-Schema serialization. Do not route the CLI's schema files through the MCP registration - it is unsupported and was never the right seam for it.

**Entry point pattern (progress-notification shape):**

```javascript
function registerPitchFeedbackTool(server, { outDir, config }) {
  server.registerTool(
    'pws_pitch_feedback',
    {
      description: 'Run the HUJI PWS_grading spine on one pitch transcript and return the Minto feedback artifact. Long-running (~8-12 min); supply _meta.progressToken to receive progress notifications.',
      inputSchema: {
        subId: z.string().min(1),
        transcriptPath: z.string().min(1),
        deckPath: z.string().optional(),
      },
      // Reuse the already-built zod/v4 contract directly - the SDK's zod-compat
      // layer (dist/cjs/server/zod-compat.js) detects `_def` (v3) vs `_zod` (v4)
      // PER SCHEMA INSTANCE independently for inputSchema and outputSchema; a v3
      // input shape next to a v4 output schema is not "mixed Zod versions" (that
      // error only fires inside ONE raw shape whose OWN field values mix
      // versions - verified in mcp.js's getZodSchemaObject/objectFromShape).
      outputSchema: FeedbackResultSchema,
    },
    async ({ subId, transcriptPath, deckPath }, extra) => {
      const progressToken = extra._meta && extra._meta.progressToken; // opt-in, per RequestMetaSchema - undefined unless the CALLER asked
      const emit = async (progress, total, message) => {
        if (!progressToken) return; // never throw on a client that did not opt in (Pitfall 10)
        try {
          await extra.sendNotification({
            method: 'notifications/progress',
            params: { progressToken, progress, total, message },
          });
        } catch (_e) { /* progress is best-effort; must never fail the tool call */ }
      };

      await emit(0, 3, 'scaffolding scratch room');
      const result = await runOneAsync({
        subId, transcriptPath, deckPath: deckPath || null, outDir, config,
        onStage: (stage) => emit(
          stage === 'stageA' ? 1 : 2, 3,
          stage === 'stageA' ? 'Stage A: extracting evidence (haiku)' : 'Stage B: grading spine (opus, up to ~15 min)'
        ),
      });
      await emit(3, 3, 'done');

      if (!result.ok) {
        return { content: [{ type: 'text', text: JSON.stringify(result) }], isError: true };
      }
      const structured = JSON.parse(fs.readFileSync(result.resultPath, 'utf8')).structured_output;
      return { content: [{ type: 'text', text: JSON.stringify(structured) }], structuredContent: structured };
    }
  );
}
```

**Model Configuration:**

Three-tier routing, all through the existing `model-profiles.cjs` cascade (no second routing brain). Current lineup and pricing per the July 2026 platform docs:

| Stage | Model (pin full ID at batch start) | Why | $/MTok in / out |
|-------|-----------------------------------|-----|-----------------|
| Extraction adapter (transcript -> evidence JSON) | `claude-haiku-4-5` | Mechanical schema-fill, no judgment; cheapest tier | $1 / $5 |
| Feedback spine (deep-grade -> mullins -> build-thesis -> structure-argument) | `claude-opus-4-8` (matches `grading` quality tier = opus) | The demo IS the sale; grading quality is the product | $5 / $25 |

Parameters: the headless CLI does not expose `temperature` - reproducibility comes from the pinned model ID + frozen prompts + frozen rubric file, not sampling params. Per-session fuses: `--max-turns 40`, `--max-budget-usd 3.00` (hard stop well under the $4-5 unit ceiling), `--no-session-persistence`, spawn `timeout` 20 min. **Core Pattern:**

Extraction-then-generation, one headless session per submission, batch loop outside.

```javascript
// Stage A - EXTRACTION (cheap model, --bare, no plugin needed):
// transcript in, evidence JSON out, validated by --json-schema at the CLI
// and re-validated by zod in the orchestrator (belt + suspenders).
const extract = spawnSync('claude', [
  '--bare', '-p',                                  // bare: no plugin discovery, fast start,
  `Extract pitch evidence from ${transcriptPath}`, // deterministic across machines
  '--model', CONFIG.extractModel,                  // claude-haiku-4-5, pinned
  '--allowedTools', 'Read',
  '--output-format', 'json',
  '--json-schema', evidenceSchemaJson,             // structured_output conforms or CLI retries
], { encoding: 'utf8', timeout: 5 * 60 * 1000 });

// Stage B - GENERATION (plugin session, one per submission):
// the PWS_grading recipe runs INSIDE the session on the runChain spine;
// the evidence JSON + scratch room are its inputs. See Section 3 entry point.
// Chain order (navigator-locked, methodology-native):
//   deep-grade -> mullins -> build-thesis (scored, non-gating) -> structure-argument (pyramid)
// Each step reads the prior step's artifact from the scratch room (stage handoff
// stays frontmatter-scanning, exactly how the commands already talk to each other).
```

Why split A from B: the extraction output is the *evidence contract* every spine command consumes (sweep 4: "output contracts are already feedback-shaped; inputs are the problem"). Extracting once on haiku and feeding all four commands beats letting each opus step re-read the raw transcript - cheaper, and every command grades the SAME evidence (consistency across the chain).

**Stage A specification - Claims-Aware Fusion intake (NORMATIVE, part of this spec):**

Stage A is not a generic "extract evidence" prompt. It is the port of the navigator's proven Claims-Aware Presentation Fusion & Analysis Engine, filed verbatim-with-ruling at `assets/claims-fusion-engine-prompt.md` (same phase directory). That file is INCLUDED BY REFERENCE in this AI-SPEC; the planner treats it as the Stage A prompt-engineering baseline. Binding rules of the port:

1. **Input contract:** submission bundle = transcript (required) + deck/paper (optional). Mode detection per the engine: transcript+deck -> Mode A COMPLETE FUSION (match transcript segments to slides, preserve exact text from both sources, mark claim gaps and contradictions); transcript-only -> extraction WITHOUT slide construction.
2. **Modes B and C are DISABLED for assessment use** (ruling stamped in the asset file). The engine never generates a transcript from slides or slides from a transcript - grading generated content is domain failure mode #1 (fabricated critique, Section 1b). A missing artifact is NAMED in the feedback as a gap, never filled.
3. **Claims-first discipline ports whole:** claim hierarchy before anything else; every extracted evidence item links to a claim; evidence chains tracked across slides/segments; "potential loss risk" markers become extraction-confidence fields in the evidence JSON.
4. **Extraction standards port whole:** every name, citation, URL, organization, statistic - captured into the evidence JSON schema (`--json-schema`-validated at the CLI, zod-revalidated in the orchestrator).
5. **Room-builder duty (navigator ruling 3):** Stage A's output populates the ephemeral room the way the shipped file-meeting pipeline populates a real room - Claimify 4-pass (selection -> disambiguation -> decomposition -> typing) through `navigation.writeClaimNode`, typed claim nodes with `review_status`, wisdom-nugget extraction. The scratch room is genuinely populated BEFORE the Stage B chain starts, so every spine command finds the room structure it was written to expect. Reuse the file-meeting machinery; do not reimplement it.
6. **Cross-artifact grading rule:** a claim is graded on its strongest presentation across artifacts (evidence shown on a slide counts even when fumbled verbally). Contradictions BETWEEN deck and speech are surfaced as feedback items, not silently resolved.
7. **Cost note:** Mode A fusion on a deck+transcript pair is a larger Stage A than transcript-only; still haiku-tier work (mechanical matching + extraction). Budget check at plan time: fused Stage A must stay within the $3.00 per-unit fuse alongside Stage B.

**Brain posture (NORMATIVE, navigator ruling 15.7.2026): READ-ONLY enrichment, generic handles only.**

- Scoring is LOCAL. The Stage B spine CONSULTS the Brain in read mode for enrichment: calibration anchors, rubric exemplars, framework weighting, methodology guidance - via `brain_ask` / `brain_search` exactly as `agents/grading.md` already does.
- **Query hygiene is the whole game:** a read whose query payload contains student-specific strings (venture names, pitch content, quotes, identifiers) is a Part 8 breach even though the operation is a read. Every Brain query in this module carries GENERIC methodology handles only ("calibration anchors for venture assessment", "rubric weighting for early-stage technology validation"). Enrichment responses land locally; fusion with student content happens on the local side of the wire.
- **Zero writes.** The module never writes to Brain. Explicitly out of pilot scope: filing graded HUJI submissions as new Brain calibration anchors - that is student content crossing the boundary and requires a separate, explicit consent agreement with HUJI if ever pursued; it is NOT a default and NOT part of Phase 229.
- **Tier 0 degradation:** Brain unreachable -> spine runs on the local anchor corpus (`calibration/` fixtures, 12 real graded reviews) with the same rubric; the result is labeled `calibration_source: local-anchors` vs `brain+local-anchors` in result.json so cohort consistency is auditable.

**Tool Use:**

No MCP servers, no Brain wire (Canon Part 8: scoring is local; the pilot runs Tier 0 rubric-grounded, Brain calibration handles are a v2 question). Tool surface per session, locked down via `--permission-mode dontAsk` + explicit allows:

- Stage A: `--allowedTools "Read"` only.
- Stage B: `--allowedTools "Read,Write,Edit,Bash(node lib/core/*)"` - enough for the chain to write artifacts into the scratch room and call the shared core; nothing else executes. Network tools stay denied (student content cannot leak via a stray WebFetch).
- Preflight (once per batch): a single `stream-json` run asserting `system/init` `plugins` contains the plugin and `plugin_errors` is absent; fail the batch closed otherwise.

**State Management:**

Filesystem is the source of truth (repo convention), three layers:

1. **Batch ledger** (`batch-state.json`): `{ subId: pending | running | done | failed, attempts, cost }`, written atomically (write-temp-then-rename) after every submission. Resume = reload ledger, skip `done`.
2. **Idempotency marker** (`out/<id>/.done`): written ONLY after zod validation of `structured_output` passes and `feedback.md` exists and is non-empty. A crash between session-exit and marker-write reruns the submission - safe, because each submission is isolated in its own scratch room (rerun = overwrite, no partial-state poisoning).
3. **Scratch room per submission** (`rooms/<id>/`): scaffolded fresh from a template (STATE.md `Stage: Validation`, `.config.json` model overrides), deleted or archived after `.done`. Sessions never share a room, so no write-lock contention and no cross-student bleed.

Retry policy: 2 retries per submission on transient failure (non-zero exit, `is_error: true`, zod validation failure), fresh scratch room each attempt, then mark `failed` and continue the loop. `failures.md` lists every failed submission with the truncated stderr and the last `session_id` - the partial-failure report is a deliverable, not a log.

**Context Window Strategy:**

The inputs are tiny (a ~2-minute pitch transcript is 1-3k tokens; the evidence JSON is smaller). The context risk is the OTHER direction: each headless session loads plugin context (commands, skills, CLAUDE.md includes) that dwarfs the student content. Strategy:

- One session per submission, chain runs inside it - the session's context grows across the 4 chain steps but stays far below any limit (~100-250k cumulative input, mostly plugin scaffolding).
- Never `--continue` across submissions - that is how student A's pitch leaks into student B's feedback. Session isolation IS the context strategy.
- Keep the loaded prefix IDENTICAL across all 200 sessions (same plugin checkout, same rubric file, `--exclude-dynamic-system-prompt-sections` to strip machine-specific prompt sections) - this is what makes prompt caching bite; see 4b.5.
- No compaction handling needed: sessions are short-lived and single-purpose; if a session ever approaches compaction, `--max-turns` kills it first and the retry runs clean.

```javascript
'use strict';
const fs = require('node:fs');
const path = require('node:path');
const util = require('node:util');
const { execFile } = require('node:child_process');
const execFileAsync = util.promisify(execFile);

const {
  scaffoldScratchRoom, buildStageAPrompt, buildStageAArgs, buildStageBArgs,
  parseEnvelope, renderFeedbackMarkdown, runGuardrails, resolveConfig,
} = require('./huji-run-one.cjs'); // reuse before build (Canon Part 7) - zero re-implementation of the spine

// execFile REJECTS the promise on a non-zero exit; spawnSync RETURNS a `.status`
// field (Pitfall 8). This helper reconstructs runOne's {status, stdout, stderr,
// error} shape so every downstream `if (res.status !== 0)` check ported below
// stays byte-identical to the sync version - only the process primitive changed.
async function runClaudeAsync(args, opts) {
  try {
    const { stdout, stderr } = await execFileAsync('claude', args, opts);
    return { status: 0, stdout, stderr, error: null };
  } catch (e) {
    return {
      status: typeof e.code === 'number' ? e.code : 1,
      stdout: e.stdout || '',
      stderr: e.stderr || String(e.message || e),
      error: e.killed ? e : null,
    };
  }
}

async function runOneAsync(opts) {
  const o = opts || {};
  const { subId, transcriptPath, outDir } = o;
  const deckPath = o.deckPath || null;
  const onStage = typeof o.onStage === 'function' ? o.onStage : () => {}; // MCP progress hook (Section 3)
  const config = resolveConfig(o.config);

  const unitOut = path.join(outDir, subId);
  fs.mkdirSync(unitOut, { recursive: true });
  const roomsDir = config.roomsDir || path.join(outDir, '..', 'rooms');
  const roomDir = path.join(roomsDir, subId);

  const scaf = scaffoldScratchRoom({ roomDir, config });
  if (!scaf.ok) return { ok: false, reason: 'scaffold_failed', detail: scaf, roomDir, outDir: unitOut };

  onStage('stageA');
  const stageAPrompt = buildStageAPrompt(config, subId, transcriptPath, deckPath);
  const aRes = await runClaudeAsync(
    buildStageAArgs(config, stageAPrompt),
    { env: process.env, encoding: 'utf8', maxBuffer: 16 * 1024 * 1024, timeout: config.stageATimeoutMs }
  );
  if (aRes.error) return { ok: false, reason: 'stageA_spawn_failed', roomDir };
  if (aRes.status !== 0) return { ok: false, reason: 'stageA_nonzero', detail: String(aRes.stderr).slice(0, 500), roomDir };
  // ... evidence parse (parseEnvelope), write evidence.json, populateRoom(evidence) -
  //     identical to runOne's Stage A body, elided here for brevity ...

  onStage('stageB');
  const bRes = await runClaudeAsync(
    buildStageBArgs(config),
    { cwd: roomDir, env: process.env, encoding: 'utf8', maxBuffer: 32 * 1024 * 1024, timeout: config.stageBTimeoutMs }
  );
  // ... feedback parse (parseEnvelope), renderFeedbackMarkdown, write feedback.md +
  //     result.json, runGuardrails(...), write .done ONLY when gate.ok - identical
  //     to runOne's Stage B body, elided here for brevity ...

  return { ok: true /* gate_failures, cost, roomDir, outDir, evidencePath, feedbackPath, resultPath, donePath - same shape as runOne */ };
}

module.exports = { runOneAsync };
```

**Progress notifications - the MCP SDK ^1.29.0 mechanics, verified against the installed package.** `notifications/progress` (`ProgressNotificationSchema`) is the correct out-of-band channel: `{ progressToken, progress, total, message }`, sent via the tool handler's second argument, `extra.sendNotification(...)`. `progressToken` is supplied by the CALLER in the tool-call request's `_meta.progressToken` (`RequestMetaSchema`) - it is opt-in per call, not a server-side setting; `extra._meta.progressToken` is `undefined` when the caller did not ask for progress. Critically, the MCP SDK's default per-request timeout is 60 seconds (`DEFAULT_REQUEST_TIMEOUT_MSEC`) and is entirely CLIENT-controlled - a client must set `resetTimeoutOnProgress: true` (or a larger `timeout`/`maxTotalTimeout`) on its own request for progress notifications to keep an 8-12 min call alive at all (Pitfall 9). The server cannot force this.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

> Written by `gsd-ai-researcher`. Cross-cutting patterns every developer building AI systems needs — independent of framework choice.

### Structured Outputs with Pydantic

This is a CJS stack - the Pydantic role is played by **zod** (`^3.25.76`, already vendored as the MCP SDK's validator). Same contract: the LLM output validates against a declared schema or the run retries; nothing unvalidated reaches a student.

```javascript
// lib/core/pitch-feedback-schemas.cjs (or phase-local) - the evidence contract
const { z } = require('zod');

const EvidenceSchema = z.object({
  submission_id: z.string().min(1),
  problem_claim: z.object({
    stated: z.boolean(),
    quote: z.string(),                       // verbatim from transcript - anti-hallucination anchor
    timestamp: z.string().regex(/^\d+:\d{2}$/).nullable(),
  }),
  value_proposition: z.object({ stated: z.boolean(), quote: z.string() }),
  evidence_claims: z.array(z.object({
    claim: z.string(),
    quote: z.string(),                       // every claim carries its transcript quote
    evidenced: z.enum(['evidenced', 'asserted', 'absent']),  // sample 1's "smart light sensor" = asserted
  })),
  self_identified_gaps: z.array(z.string()), // sample 2 names its own gaps - REWARD metacognition,
                                             // never double-punish (CONTEXT eval note)
  speaker_count: z.number().int().positive(),
  language_notes: z.string(),                // non-native English - feedback must be language-gentle
});

const FeedbackResultSchema = z.object({
  submission_id: z.string(),
  governing_thought: z.string().min(20),     // Minto: pyramid top, first
  branches: z.array(z.object({
    point: z.string(),
    support: z.array(z.string()).min(1),
    teachable_next_step: z.string(),         // Canon Part 12: formative, teachable order
  })).min(2).max(3),                          // 2-3 branches at the student's level (navigator ruling)
  scores: z.record(z.string(), z.number().min(0).max(100)), // per-dimension, rubric-tiered
  model_id: z.string(),                       // provenance: which pinned model graded this student
});
```

**Two validation layers, deliberately redundant:**
1. **CLI layer:** the same schema, serialized to JSON Schema, passed as `--json-schema`. Claude Code validates and self-corrects internally before returning; the conforming object lands in the `structured_output` field of the JSON envelope. (zod 3.25.x ships `require('zod/v4').z.toJSONSchema(schema)` for this serialization - verify the CJS subpath at build time; fallback is hand-writing the two small JSON Schema files.)
2. **Orchestrator layer:** `Schema.safeParse(out.structured_output)` in the batch loop. This catches CLI-version regressions (pre-2.1.205 the flag failed silently) and envelope corruption.

**Retry logic:** on `safeParse` failure - 2 retries max, fresh scratch room per attempt. Log per attempt: `submission_id`, attempt number, `session_id`, the zod issue paths (`result.error.issues.map(i => i.path.join('.'))`), and `total_cost_usd` spent on the dud. Surface (stop the batch, page the human) only when the FAILURE RATE crosses ~5% over a 20-submission window - that is a systemic prompt/schema bug, not noise; individual failures just land in `failures.md`.

### Async-First Design

Node async here means **process concurrency, not promise gymnastics**: each submission is a spawned `claude` child process; the orchestrator's job is pool management.

- Use `child_process.spawn` (async, args array, no shell) with a concurrency pool of 3-4; collect exit via promises. A dependency-free pool is ~15 lines: maintain a `running` set, launch while `running.size < LIMIT`, on each `close` event launch the next pending submission.
- **The one common mistake in this stack:** `execSync` in a loop (the `label-topic-forest.cjs` precedent). It blocks the event loop, serializes 200 submissions into ~30 hours of wall time, and its string-interpolated shell quoting breaks on apostrophes in transcripts. This is the CJS equivalent of `asyncio.run()` inside an event loop: the tool exists, and using it here is always wrong.
- **Stream vs. await:** await (plain `--output-format json`) for both stages - structured output must be validated whole before anything is written, and nobody is watching a batch stream at 2am. `stream-json` is reserved for exactly two spots: the one-time preflight (reading `system/init` plugin fields) and optional progress telemetry (counting `system/api_retry` events to detect rate-limit pressure and auto-drop concurrency).

### Prompt Engineering Discipline

- **System vs. user separation:** the rubric, tier configuration (which of the ten questions this course teaches, at what depth - the HUJI calibration workshop output), and tone constitution (Part 12: never grade-and-compliment theater) live in `--append-system-prompt-file rubric-huji.md`. The transcript and evidence JSON are user content. This split is also the cache boundary: system-side is identical across 200 runs (cacheable), user-side varies per student.
- **Few-shot:** inline, static, exactly two - the two Amnon samples (safescan, study-app) with their approved demo feedback artifacts, embedded in the rubric file once the demo is approved. No dynamic retrieval: N=200 of one assignment type from one course needs zero example routing, and static few-shot keeps the cached prefix bit-identical. The samples double as the calibration anchor: sample 2 demonstrates rewarding self-identified gaps, sample 1 demonstrates flagging asserted-not-evidenced tech claims.
- **Token bounds:** the headless CLI does not expose `max_tokens` directly - the equivalent production bounds are `--max-turns 40` (agentic-loop bound) and `--max-budget-usd 3.00` (spend bound), both set on EVERY spawn, never left off. In the schema itself, bound the free-text fields (`z.string().max(...)` on branch points and next steps) so feedback length is a contract, not a hope - a 4,000-word pyramid is a pedagogical failure for a 2-minute pitch.

### Context Window Management

Covered structurally in Section 4 (session isolation IS the strategy); the cross-cutting rules:

- **Isolation over summarization.** This hybrid pipeline never needs conversation summarization because no conversation survives a submission. The failure mode to design against is accidental context sharing: no `--continue`, no `--resume` across submissions, no shared scratch room, no batch-wide "notes" file readable by grading sessions.
- **Evidence JSON as the compression layer.** The 4-step chain passes the ~1-2k-token evidence object forward, not the raw transcript re-read four times. If v2 adds slide frames (open question 1), vision output compresses into the same evidence contract - the spine never sees images, so the context and cost model survive the v2 upgrade.
- **Prefix stability as a managed asset.** The plugin checkout, rubric file, and few-shot block are frozen for the duration of the batch (git tag the checkout). Any mid-batch edit both invalidates the prompt cache AND changes grading conditions across the cohort - treat the frozen prefix as part of the grade's provenance.

- **Caching strategy:** Claude Code manages `cache_control` itself; the orchestrator's lever is prefix stability (above) + run cadence. Cache TTL is 5 minutes - a continuously-fed pool keeps it warm for the whole batch (reads at 0.1x input price = the bulk of the warm-column savings); a paused-and-resumed batch eats one cache re-write (1.25x) per resume, which is noise. Confirm caching is actually biting by checking the first few `result.json` cost figures against the cold estimate.

Two flags if ever adopted: batches are NOT Zero-Data-Retention eligible (weigh against the FERPA/privacy story being sold to a university - note that Part 8 governs the Mindrian Brain boundary, and student transcripts already transit the Anthropic API in every design), and `max_tokens: 0` cache pre-warming is not supported inside a batch. Verdict for the pilot: stay on headless CLI; the unit economics do not need the discount.

### Rubrics (domain language, anchored in the on-disk assets)

**D1 - Transcript-evidence grounding (Critical)**
> PASS: Every substantive claim in the feedback points at something the student actually said, via verbatim quote or timestamp. SafeScan's "smart light sensor" is flagged as asserted-not-evidenced BY CITING the student's own 1:10-region line. Sample 2's risks+mitigation section (0:52) is credited as covered.
> FAIL: Feedback praises, criticizes, or quotes anything absent from the transcript; flags "missing risks" on sample 2; any quoted string in evidence.json or feedback.md that does not appear (whitespace/case-normalized) in the source transcript.
> Measurement: Code first - a CJS quote verifier asserts every `evidence_claims[].quote` and every quoted span in feedback.md exists verbatim in the transcript. LLM Judge second - does every feedback claim trace to an evidence.json item. Human third - Jonathan samples per Section 7.

**D2 - Intake fidelity (Critical)**
> PASS: Stage A output contains 100% of the hand-labeled entity/claim inventory for the fixture (every name, citation, URL, organization, statistic, claim - the fusion-engine extraction standard). Sample 1 inventory includes: big-nine allergen groups, 3-step usage, 18-month shelf-life target, FDA compliance mention, both hires. Sample 2 includes: 3 features, both risks WITH mitigations, 10-12 week timeline, 3 roles, 3 self-named gaps.
> FAIL: Any inventory item absent from evidence.json; a self-identified gap (sample 2, 1:45) not captured in `self_identified_gaps`; Modes B/C output detected (any evidence item not traceable to a source artifact).
> Measurement: Code - recall check against the labeled inventories (Jonathan labels the 2 samples + first 10 real submissions during implementation).

**D3 - Cohort score consistency (Critical)**
> PASS: Duplicate-anchor probes (samples 1 and 2 re-injected under alias IDs at batch positions 1/50/100/150/200) score within 1 band and produce materially identical branch verdicts across all 5 positions. Rolling cohort score mean/stdev per 25 units shows no monotonic drift. All 200 result.json envelopes carry the identical pinned `model_id`.
> FAIL: Any probe deviates > 1 band from its position-1 run; two near-duplicate pitches (fairness probe pair) receive visibly different severity; any `model_id` mismatch.
> Measurement: Code - probe injection + drift stats in the eval harness. Human - Jonathan compares the 5 probe artifacts side by side once per batch.

**D4 - Part 8 query hygiene (Critical)**
> PASS: Every `brain_ask` / `brain_search` payload in the batch's Brain-query log contains ONLY generic methodology handles ("calibration anchors for venture assessment"). The log scans clean against `part8-egress-guard.cjs` AND against the per-submission entity inventory from evidence.json (venture names, quotes, distinctive terms).
> FAIL: One student-specific string (venture name, quote fragment, identifier) in one query payload. A read with student content in it is a breach even though the operation is a read.
> Measurement: Code - reuse `lib/core/part8-egress-guard.cjs` (`scanForContent`/`classify`) plus a per-submission inventory grep over the logged payloads. Zero tolerance.

**D5 - Minto/MECE structure validity (High)**
> PASS: `FeedbackResultSchema` validates (governing thought >= 20 chars first, 2-3 branches, each with point + support + teachable_next_step, bounded lengths). Judge confirms branches are MECE at the student's level and the governing thought actually governs.
> FAIL: Schema violation; branches that overlap or leave the pitch's main weakness uncovered; a 4,000-word essay; verdict-without-path branches ("market analysis is weak").
> Measurement: Code (zod, both validation layers per 4b) + LLM Judge for MECE-ness.

**D6 - Part 12 tone: formative, language-gentle, metacognition rewarded (High)**
> PASS: Sample 2's self-named gaps ("deeper market research, competitor analysis, user testing") are explicitly credited as demonstrated self-assessment skill and DEEPENED (how to run the competitor analysis), never re-listed as discovered deficiencies. Disfluencies ("vali- validating", "uh"), diarization noise ("Recording in progress" as Speaker 1), and non-native phrasing ("Restaurant kitchen cannot guarantee") are never surfaced as weaknesses. No grade-and-compliment theater; no praise inflation telling a weak pitch it is strong.
> FAIL: Double-punished metacognition; feedback reading as language correction; empty compliments sandwiching harsh verdicts.
> Measurement: LLM Judge with explicit violation checklist, calibrated per below; Human review of every unit where `self_identified_gaps` is non-empty and the judge flags overlap without crediting language.

**D7 - Course-tier calibration + actionability (High)**
> PASS: Every branch carries the Hattie & Timperley triple - the criterion (feed-up), what THIS pitch did against it with evidence (feed-back), and ONE concrete next step doable before the next milestone (feed-forward), e.g. "run 5 interviews with allergic diners to test the 'afraid to trust the waiter' claim." Depth matches the course skeleton (problem -> value -> prototype -> risks+mitigation -> critical path -> team -> gaps), tiered per the HUJI calibration-workshop rubric.
> FAIL: Investor-gauntlet demands (unit economics, moat, defensibility, term sheets, CAC/LTV) the assignment never asked for; next steps the student cannot execute in a semester; feedback that only summarizes the pitch.
> Measurement: LLM Judge (calibrated); Human - the TA blind comparison is the ground-truth check for this dimension.

**D8 - Anti-templating / individuation (High)**
> PASS: Pairwise shingle-Jaccard similarity between any two delivered feedback.md files stays below threshold (start at 0.5, tune on the first 10); the swap test passes - a judge given feedback X against mismatched transcript Y detects the mismatch.
> FAIL: Two artifacts a reader could swap between students without noticing; cohort-wide convergence to one template with names substituted. The instructor reads many artifacts side by side and detects interchangeability faster than any single student.
> Measurement: Code - dependency-free CJS shingle-similarity pass over all delivered artifacts. LLM Judge - swap test on sampled pairs.

**D9 - Per-unit cost adherence (High)**
> PASS: Every result.json `total_cost_usd` <= $3.00; warm-cache average <= $2.00; batch total tracks the Section 4b projection ($150-400).
> FAIL: The `--max-budget-usd` fuse tripping on > 2% of units; warm units costing cold prices (cache not biting).
> Measurement: Code - the cost ledger comes free in every envelope.

**D10 - Batch resume / isolation correctness (High)**
> PASS: Kill the orchestrator mid-batch, restart: `done` units skipped, `running` units re-run clean in fresh scratch rooms, no artifact double-written. Isolation probe: grep student A's distinctive entity strings (from A's evidence.json) across all other students' feedback.md - zero hits.
> FAIL: A resumed batch re-grading finished students (cost + drift risk); any cross-student string hit; `.done` written before all gates pass.
> Measurement: Code - harness tests in `tests/run-all-229.sh` (kill/resume scenario + cross-bleed grep).

**D11 - MCP job lifecycle correctness / fire-and-poll (Critical)**
> PASS: Every `pws_pitch_feedback` fire call returns a job_id (= subId) that maps to exactly one on-disk job record (`outDir/<id>/`); a `pws_pitch_feedback_status` poll against an in-flight, completed, failed, or unknown job_id ALWAYS returns a well-formed `{status: running|done|failed|not_found}` response, never a throw or a hang; a job whose caller never polls again still runs to completion and its artifacts remain on disk under the batch's normal retention, without leaking a still-running `claude` child process or an un-reclaimed scratch room past job completion.
> FAIL: two concurrent fire calls reusing the same subId race or silently overwrite the same `outDir/<id>/` record; a status poll on a subId that was never fired throws an exception or blocks instead of returning `not_found`; an abandoned (never-polled) job leaves its Stage A/B child process or scratch room present indefinitely with no bound - "the client never polls" degrading into an unbounded resource leak rather than a merely-unread result.
> Measurement: Code - extend `tests/run-all-229.sh` with (a) a job-id-collision fixture: fire two `runOneAsync` calls with the same subId concurrently, assert one is rejected (G9) rather than silently corrupting the other's record; (b) an unknown-job-id fixture: poll a job_id that was never fired, assert a clean `not_found`, never a throw; (c) an abandoned-job fixture: fire, never poll, assert the job still reaches `.done`/`failed` and its scratch room is reclaimed per the existing retention rule (Section 4 State Management), not orphaned. Fully code-checkable state-machine behavior; no human/judge pass needed.

**D12 - Standalone-call cost/concurrency ceiling adherence (High)**
> PASS: Cumulative daily call count and cumulative daily spend across ALL standalone `pws_pitch_feedback` invocations (everything NOT tagged as running inside `huji-batch.cjs`) stay under the configured ledger thresholds (default: 20 calls/day, $75/day - roughly 25 units at the existing $3.00 per-unit `--max-budget-usd` fuse from Section 4). A call that would breach either threshold is refused BEFORE Stage A spawns, with `isError: true` and the current count/spend + threshold in the message. Batch-orchestrator calls are tagged and exempt, so the pilot's planned 200-unit overnight run is never blocked by this guardrail.
> FAIL: the ledger undercounts because of a non-atomic write (a crash mid-increment silently loses a call, bypassing the ceiling); two concurrent standalone calls both read the pre-increment count before either writes and race past the threshold (TOCTOU); the (N+1)th call over the daily cap is allowed to actually run Stage A/B and only fails afterward (refusal must be pre-spawn, not a post-hoc write-off); batch-orchestrator calls get wrongly counted against the standalone ceiling and the 200-unit pilot run halts partway through on its own guardrail.
> Measurement: Code - a rapid-successive-calls harness test that drives the ledger past 20/day and asserts call #21 is refused pre-spawn (assert the `claude` spawn primitive was never invoked for the refused call); a write-temp-then-rename crash-recovery test (kill the process mid-ledger-write, reload, assert the count is exactly what was durably committed); a batch-vs-standalone tagging test (batch-orchestrator calls do not increment the standalone ledger).

**D13 - Rubric/course-context required-field enforcement (Critical)**
> PASS: A `pws_pitch_feedback` call that supplies a valid `rubricContext` (v1 allowlist: exactly `"huji-intro-venture"`) runs normally. A call that OMITS `rubricContext`, or supplies any other value, is rejected at the input-validation layer BEFORE Stage A spawns, with `isError: true` and a message naming the missing/invalid field - never a silent default to HUJI's intro-course tiering for an unrelated transcript or a future non-HUJI customer's actual investor pitch (Section 1b's Critical "rubric/course-context binding explicitness" finding, the exact inverse of the base 6-component rubric's "grading a pitch is an anti-pattern" failure mode).
> FAIL: a call with `rubricContext` omitted proceeds and silently grades under `rubric-huji.md`'s intro-course calibration anyway; the field exists in the zod input shape but is marked `.optional()` or carries a default (making the "required" claim cosmetic); rejection happens AFTER Stage A/B already ran (wasted spend, not a real gate, and the transcript already left the machine to Stage A's `claude` process under the wrong tier).
> Measurement: Code - a synthetic fixture that calls the tool handler (or `runOneAsync` directly) with `rubricContext` omitted, asserting (a) the call is rejected, (b) rejection happens before any `claude` child process spawns (assert on a spy/mock that `runClaudeAsync`/Stage A args were never built), (c) the error names the missing field. A second fixture supplies an invalid non-allowlisted value (e.g. `"investor-pitch"`) and asserts the same fail-closed behavior. This is the concrete "must fail closed, not silently default" fixture the guardrail exists to prove.

**D14 - Async/sync exit-code parity, execFile regression guard (High)**
> PASS: A synthetic Stage A or Stage B fixture that exits non-zero produces a structurally identical `{ ok: false, reason: 'stageA_nonzero' | 'stageB_nonzero', detail: <stderr slice>, ... }` envelope under BOTH `runOne` (sync, `spawnSync`, returns a `.status` field) and `runOneAsync` (async, `execFile`-promisified, REJECTS the promise on non-zero exit - Section 4's Pitfall 8) - the two engines are provably interchangeable on the FAILURE path, not just the happy path.
> FAIL: `runOneAsync`'s `runClaudeAsync` wrapper lets a rejected `execFileAsync` promise propagate as an uncaught exception instead of reconstructing `{status, stdout, stderr, error}`; the reconstructed shape uses a different key name, a different reason-code string, or drops `stderr` such that `stageA_nonzero`/`stageB_nonzero` stops matching what `failures.md` and `--dry-run` assertions already depend on; the sync and async engines diverge on ANY field of the failure envelope for the same injected non-zero exit.
> Measurement: Code - a parity test mirroring `lib/memory/sync-async-entry-points.test.cjs`'s own shape: stub the child-process primitive (`spawnSync`/`execFile`) to exit non-zero with fixed stdout/stderr for both `runOne` and `runOneAsync`, run both, assert the two returned objects are structurally identical modulo timing-only fields. This is a CI-blocking regression gate protecting the async rewrite itself (extend `tests/run-all-229.sh` or add a sibling `.test.cjs`), not a runtime guardrail - there is no G-number for D14 because nothing here intervenes on a live request; it fails a build before the async path ever ships.

**D15 - Deployment-scope invocation traceability (High)**
> PASS (v1, minimal): every `pws_pitch_feedback` invocation's `rubricContext` (D13/G8's required field) is appended to a local, durable invocation audit trail (write-temp-then-rename, the same pattern `batch-state.json` already uses) alongside subId and timestamp - so "which rubric, which declared context, when" is reconstructable per invocation without inferring it from "who currently has the MCP server wired up" (Section 1b's "deployment-scope integrity" finding).
> FAIL (v1, minimal): an invocation runs with no trace of which context it claimed to operate under; the audit log is missing, non-atomic (can be corrupted or partially written by a crash mid-write), or silently overwritten rather than append-only.
> Measurement: Code - assert every `runOneAsync` call writes an audit-log line before Stage A spawns; a kill-mid-write test asserts the log stays append-only and uncorrupted on reload.
> **Accepted gap (v1, explicitly out of scope):** this design proves an invocation DECLARED a scope; it does not prove the CALLER was AUTHORIZED to declare it - there is no per-caller identity/auth binding, no cryptographic attestation, and no server-side allowlist of which MCP clients may reach the tool at all (today's boundary is informal: "nobody but Jonathan has the MCP server connected"). Full FERPA "direct control" enforcement is deferred; see G10 below for the same gap stated as a guardrail limitation, with owner and revisit trigger.

## 6. Guardrails

> "Online" for this batch system means: per-submission gates in the orchestrator, enforced BEFORE `.done` is written. Nothing that fails a gate is ever delivered to a student - the `.done` marker is the delivery contract. Two triggers escalate from unit-block to whole-batch halt because they mean the MECHANISM is broken, not the unit: a Part 8 hit and a model-provenance mismatch. Delivery itself is human-gated: the batch output goes to Jonathan's sampling pass (Section 7), then Amnon, before any student sees anything.

### Online (Per-Submission, pre-`.done`)

| Guardrail | Trigger | Intervention |
|-----------|---------|--------------|
| G1 Quote-grounding gate | Any quote in evidence.json or any quoted span in feedback.md not found verbatim (whitespace/case-normalized) in the source transcript | Block unit -> retry (max 2, fresh scratch room) -> mark `failed`, log to failures.md |
| G2 Schema gate | zod `safeParse` failure on `structured_output` at either stage (belt-and-suspenders layer from Section 4b) | Block -> retry -> `failed` |
| G3 Part 8 egress gate | `part8-egress-guard.cjs` classify hit on any logged Brain query payload, OR any string from the unit's evidence.json entity inventory found in any query payload | **HALT ENTIRE BATCH** + page human. Constitutional breach - one leak means the hygiene mechanism is broken for all 200 |
| G4 Model provenance gate | `result.json` `model_id` differs from the pinned ID in batch.config.json | Block unit + **HALT BATCH** - cohort fairness is broken (Pitfall 1) |
| G5 Cost fuse | `--max-budget-usd 3.00` CLI hard stop, re-checked against the ledger per unit | Unit killed by CLI -> retry once -> `failed`; if > 2% of units trip the fuse, pause batch (systemic, not noise) |
| G6 Length/tone bound | feedback.md exceeds schema `max()` bounds or total length > ~900 words; governing thought missing or not first | Block -> retry. A 4,000-word pyramid on a 2-minute pitch is a pedagogical failure, not a style choice |

Deliberately NOT an online gate: D6 double-punish detection and D7 tier calibration need judgment - they run offline (below) with human review before delivery, because a cheap heuristic would false-positive on legitimate gap-deepening feedback.

### Offline (Flywheel)

| Metric | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| Judge quality score (Real/Win/Worth 1-5 + D1/D6/D7 violation lists) | 100% of first 10 units; then all retried/flagged units + random 10% | Any D1 (grounding) violation in a delivered artifact -> pull artifact, re-run unit; 2+ D1 violations in a sample window -> pause batch, fix extraction/prompt |
| Severity drift | Duplicate-anchor probes at batch positions 1/50/100/150/200; rolling cohort score mean/stdev per 25 units | Probe deviates > 1 band from its position-1 run -> pause, inspect cache state / prefix / model before resuming |
| Template convergence index | Pairwise shingle-Jaccard over ALL delivered feedback.md (cheap, dependency-free CJS) | Any pair > 0.5 -> both artifacts to human review; rising cohort median -> individuation fix in the rubric prompt before continuing |
| Metacognition credit rate | 100% of units where `self_identified_gaps` is non-empty (judge checks credit-and-deepen vs re-list) | Any double-punish finding -> human review of that unit before delivery; pattern of 3+ -> rubric-file fix |
| Warm-cache cost trend | First 5 units (cold) vs rolling per-unit cost | Warm average > $2.00 -> caching not biting; check prefix stability (frozen checkout, `--exclude-dynamic-system-prompt-sections`) before burning the margin |
| External signal (signal-metric divergence watch) | Amnon's demo verdict; TA blind comparison (n >= 10); post-pilot student perceived-usefulness survey via Amnon | Human verdicts diverging from judge scores = the judge is miscalibrated, not the humans - recalibrate against the anchor protocol before the semester-license conversation |

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing Tool:** Plugin-native ledger, no external service. The trace store is what the pipeline already emits for free: per-unit `result.json` envelopes (`session_id`, `total_cost_usd` with per-model breakdown, pinned `model_id`, `structured_output`, `is_error`, `calibration_source: local-anchors | brain+local-anchors`) + `batch-state.json` (state, attempts, cost per unit) + the Brain-query log (every payload, for the G3 audit) + `failures.md`. One CJS reporter (`scripts/huji-eval.cjs --report`) renders the cohort view from these files: cost curve, score distribution, drift chart against the probes, similarity matrix, failure list. `stream-json` stays reserved for the batch preflight (plugin-load assertion) and `api_retry` rate-limit telemetry (Section 4b). If the module graduates to continuous semester-scale runs, revisit self-hosted Phoenix; it is not a pilot dependency.

**Key Metrics to Track:**
1. **Per-unit cost + cumulative spend** - `total_cost_usd` per envelope, warm-vs-cold trend, projection against the ~$150-400 batch estimate.
2. **Unit failure rate** - rolling 20-submission window (retries, zod failures, fuse trips).
3. **Grounding-gate (G1) failure rate** - how often the pipeline TRIES to fabricate; the leading indicator for domain failure mode #1 even when the gate catches it.
4. **Severity drift** - probe deviation (positions 1/50/100/150/200) + rolling cohort score stats; plus the `model_id` and `calibration_source` uniformity checks (all 200 identical).
5. **Part 8 hygiene violations** - must be identically ZERO across the whole batch; reported as an explicit "0 violations in N queries" line in the batch report (the auditable privacy story sold to HUJI).
6. **Template similarity index** - cohort pairwise max + median.
7. **MCP standalone-call ledger (v1.1)** - daily call count + daily spend for calls outside the batch orchestrator, tracked against the G7 ceiling (20 calls / $75 default).
8. **Job lifecycle backlog (v1.1)** - count of non-terminal `outDir/<id>/` records older than the orphan-sweep TTL (24h default); should be ~0 in steady state, per D11/G9.
9. **Deployment-scope audit-trail completeness (v1.1)** - every invocation has a matching G10 log line; any gap is a finding, not noise (per D15's accepted-gap boundary - the log proves declaration, not authorization).

**Alert Thresholds:**
- Failure rate >= 5% over a 20-submission window -> **stop batch** (systemic prompt/schema bug per Section 4b, not noise).
- Any G3 (Part 8) or G4 (model provenance) trigger -> **immediate batch halt + human page** (already wired as online guardrails; restated here as the two non-negotiable alerts).
- Anchor probe deviation > 1 band -> pause and inspect before resuming.
- Warm per-unit average > $2.00, or any unit > $3.00 surviving the CLI fuse -> investigate caching/prefix before continuing.
- Projected batch total > $500 -> pause; unit economics decision before spending further.
- Any post-hoc judge-detected D1 violation in a delivered artifact -> pull that artifact from the delivery set and re-run the unit (delivery is not final until the human gate below passes).
- **(v1.1) Standalone-call ledger crossing 20 calls/day or $75/day** -> already refused online (G7); restated here as an alert - a caller repeatedly hitting the ceiling is a signal to investigate (legitimate scaling need vs. a runaway/unauthorized caller), not to silently raise the threshold.
- **(v1.1) Any G8 rejection (missing/invalid `rubricContext`) or G9 collision** -> logged and reviewed; a rising rejection rate signals a misconfigured or unauthorized caller, not routine noise.
- **(v1.1) Orphaned job count > 0 at sweep time** -> investigate before assuming "client never polled" is benign; the caller may have crashed mid-flow expecting a result it never received.

**Smart Sampling Strategy (Jonathan's human-review pass, pre-delivery):**
- 100% of the first 10 units - batch shakedown before letting it run overnight.
- 100% of retried-then-passed units - a retry is a concerning signal even when the output validates.
- 100% of flagged units - similarity pairs > 0.5, metacognition-credit flags, judge score floor, length near bound.
- Weakest 3 + strongest 3 by cohort score - the edge cases Amnon and the TAs will read first; they must be right.
- Random 10% of the remainder.
- Expected review load: ~35-45 artifacts of 200 - one focused evening, budgeted as part of the pilot's delivery cost, not optional QA. Students receive feedback only after this pass + Amnon's sample review clears.
- **MCP delivery seam note (v1.1):** `pws_pitch_feedback` outputs are NOT automatically routed through this sampling pass - Section 1b's "feedback-quality consistency outside the batch-and-verify pipeline" finding already names this as the standalone tool's own quality-floor gap (same grading engine, same G1-G6, but no human gate before delivery). Nothing in G7-G10 closes that gap; it is a delivery-process discipline, not a code guardrail. Treat any student-facing or instructor-facing use of the standalone tool as requiring the identical manual human-review step the batch enforces structurally, until/unless a future revision wires the sampling pass into the tool itself.
