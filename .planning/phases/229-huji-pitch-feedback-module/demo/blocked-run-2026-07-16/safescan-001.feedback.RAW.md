# Feedback: safescan-001

SafeScan is a clearly-framed, well-sequenced pitch whose one weak link is evidence: the two moment-of-truth claims (that the sensor actually detects allergens, and that diners will act) are asserted rather than shown, and closing that evidence gap is the single highest-value next step.

## The problem and value proposition land - the frame is clear and legible in the first twenty seconds.

- Problem is concrete and filmable: "Restaurant kitchen cannot guarantee one hundred percent safe food, so diners are often afraid to trust the waiter" (transcript 0:10).
- Value proposition is immediately understood: "a tiny device that tests your food right at the table and gives you a scientific answer in less than a minute", reinforced by an evidenced three-step operation.
- Course-tier: problem-real (Q1) and differentiated (Q4) both score 1; differentiation rests on point-of-consumption speed and portability, not a defensibility claim.

Next step: Ground the 'big problem around the world' line in one observation: interview five people with food allergies about what they actually do today when they eat out, and open the pitch with what you hear.

## The core technology claim is asserted, not shown - this is the evidence gap to close first.

- Load-bearing claim is stated as fact with no demonstration: "The science inside uses smart light sensor. This technology detects even tiny traces of allergen proteins that the human eye cannot see" (evidence disposition: asserted).
- The student's own roadmap places the prototype ahead of them: "First, we'll finish our prototype and hardware validation" - so momentum (Q5) scores 0, which is expected for a first venture and flagged gently, not as a fatal flaw.
- This is the sample-1 pattern: flag the asserted-not-evidenced tech claim without demanding investor-grade proof.

Next step: Run one bench test and describe it - spike a known allergen into a sample and show the device flags it against a clean control - to move the core claim from asserted to evidenced. Course level, not FDA-grade sensitivity.

## The plan and self-awareness are genuine strengths; the remaining gap is the people and user validation.

- Roadmap is sequenced and hiring is staged to need: "biosensor engineer... a mobile app developer" now, "a safety expert... an operation manager" later, so roadmap-clear (Q6) and resources-realistic (Q7) both score 1.
- Metacognition credited, not double-punished: the student self-identified their own open questions - "expanding market analysis, and studying FDA compliance" - which is exactly the right instinct and is rewarded rather than re-listed as a deficiency.
- Gaps are team-fit (Q8=0, no founder-problem fit stated) and evidence that users will act (Q2=0, fear is asserted but no current behavior or workaround is described).

Next step: Add a single line on founder-problem fit (what draws you to allergen safety), and fold the diner interviews in to test that fear actually drives people to act.
