# The reward-before-investment guard could not see its own onboarding hook, and 67 of 113 commands were quietly undeclared

> Lands in: MindrianOS-Plugin Phase 267.3
> (.planning/phases/267.3-reward-before-investment-guard-jurisdiction/)
> and the ROADMAP Phase 267.3 entry (.planning/ROADMAP.md), which carries the phase-outcome
> paragraph, the measured numbers, and the explicit scope statement.
> Registers: GUARD-01 through GUARD-10 in .planning/REQUIREMENTS.md.
> Pinned to v2.0.0-beta.14 / main worktree-agent-a1314e5dcb0db3d05 @ ca5a42ed (phase close).

Source: `267.3-AUDIT.md` (the measured jurisdiction map), `267.3-DECISIONS.md` (the ruling
on D-A/D-B/D-C with rejected alternatives), `267.3-CLASSIFICATION.md` (the rubric and all 67
per-command rows), `docs/reward-before-investment-rule.md` (the corrected enforcement
description), and the eight plan SUMMARY files across seven waves.

## The governance finding: a linter that never saw its own front door

The reward-before-investment rule (Phase 118-06, shipped 2026-05-15) requires every
interactive flow to deliver a variable reward before asking for more than one sentence of
investment. The enforcement mechanism, `lib/core/mva-rule-linter.cjs`, reads a single
frontmatter field, `interactive_first_reward`, out of `commands/*.md` files. Four independent
proofs in the codebase converge on the same jurisdiction: the library's own header comment,
the single non-recursive `scanCommands(commandsDir)` entry point, the CLI's default target
(`path.join(__dirname, '..', 'commands')`), and the pre-commit hook's own matcher
(`git diff --cached --name-only | grep -qE '^commands/.+\.md$'`). All four say the same thing:
the guard's jurisdiction is exactly `commands/*.md`, at commit time, and only for files a
given commit happens to stage.

`scripts/session-start` is a bash script. It is the single most-first flow in the product,
the one every user hits before any command, and being a bash script means it has no
frontmatter block to carry a declaration in. Four of its branches (FIRST_INSTALL, UPDATE,
MODE_ROUTING, COLD_START_MENU) emit user-facing first-touch prose, and every one of them sat
structurally outside the guard from day one. This is not a content gap someone forgot to
fill in; it is a governance gap, an enforcement mechanism with a blind spot exactly where the
product's highest-stakes first impression lives. It is also why the other gaps in the
Hooked-model onboarding flow survived undetected for so long: nothing was ever built that
could catch them (GAP G-1, `rethinking-mindrianos/research/2026-08-27-hooked-first-install-audit/`).

## The debt ratchet: a staged-scoped gate cannot measure a repo-wide gap

The second finding is structural rather than a single gap. Phase 245-02 deliberately
narrowed the commit-time hook to `--staged` scope, correctly fixing a real problem: one
pre-existing offender was blocking every commit that touched any command file, which had
made `COMMIT_NO_VERIFY=1` mandatory rather than exceptional. That fix was locally correct.
Its side effect was structural: a file that is never staged again after the rule ships has
no mechanism left to surface its own declaration gap. Phase 118-06 shipped with 6
declarations and no backfill plan; the other ~107 commands accreted declarations
opportunistically, by accident, whenever an unrelated phase happened to touch a file. There
is no coherent pattern to which commands got declared and which did not -- direct twins land
on opposite sides (`analyze-needs` declared, `analyze-systems` missing; `find-analogies`
declared, `find-connections` missing).

Phase 271 plan 03 discovered this the hard way: an unrelated bare-reference-path sweep
staged 16 previously-untouched command files and hit the linter's block cold, stalling at
28 of 45 files with no warning that the debt existed until the exact moment it collided with
unrelated work. `DEFERRED-271-D1` names the mechanism verbatim: "The linter scans staged
`commands/*.md` only... the gap was invisible to every commit that did not happen to touch
them." At the moment Phase 267.3 measured it fresh (2026-08-27, HEAD `cf7eabf3`), 67 of 113
commands -- 59 percent -- were undeclared, and zero commits were currently blocked by that
fact. A staged-scoped gate answers "did this commit introduce new debt," never "how much
debt already exists." Those are different questions, and only one of them was being asked.

## The ruling: three linked questions, one navigator decision, no relaxation

The phase-01 plan surfaced three design questions and the navigator ruled all three as
presented, option-a, no amendments, in one blocking checkpoint before any code was written.

**D-A (the out-of-frontmatter declaration mechanism).** A new sibling registry,
`data/first-reward-surfaces.json`, modeled directly on the existing
`data/first-touch-surfaces.json` shape -- Canon Part 7 reuse-before-build applied literally,
copying a house pattern the repo already ships twice rather than minting a third parsing
surface. Two options were argued and rejected on the record: a parsed bash-comment
convention (rejected -- a second parser beside the frontmatter parser, no precedent, does
not generalize to non-bash prose surfaces) and extending the born-wired connector registry
(rejected -- that registry is generated and describes invocation wiring, not first-reward
contracts, and `scripts/session-start` is a hook, not an invocable surface, so it would not
even fit).

**D-B (the vocabulary amendment).** The closed six-member `REWARD_TYPES` vocabulary was
minted, five of six terms, against exactly one flow each (`instant_brief` for
`/mos:new-project`, `paragraph_preview` for `/mos:file-meeting`,
`calibration_distribution_preview` for `/mos:grade`, `reframe_question` for
`/mos:onboard`). It could not honestly describe ten conversational methodology commands
(the first delivered value is Larry's analysis over the user's own material, a genuinely
different shape than any of the five bound terms) or nine diagnostic/report commands
(`status`, `doctor`, `heal` and their family -- the rule doc's own words already call a
status report "not a reward," but the vocabulary gave that category no legal value to
declare). Two new members were minted with a written reason each:
`methodology_reframe` and `--none (diagnostic surface)`. A third member,
`live_deliverable`, was minted later, mid-classification, when `/mos:publish`'s live
shareable deploy URL turned out to fit none of the eight existing terms honestly -- the
classification agent's own framing was the deciding evidence: `--none (diagnostic surface)`
would have been "false about publish in a different direction," since publish performs an
irreversible action and hands back a real artifact, which is categorically not "reporting
state." A rubber-stamped `--none` there would have defeated the whole phase's purpose.

**D-C (the lint scope).** The commit gate stayed `--staged`, exactly as Phase 245-02 built
it -- widening it back would re-create the forced-bypass problem that fix solved. The
whole-tree audit (already the CLI's default mode, already exiting 1) got wired into
`scripts/verify-release` as a fail-closed RELEASE gate instead, on a two-step sequence
matching the 271-05 discipline: wire the gate first (plan 267.3-03's registry-scoped
`--surfaces` gate, wave 3, when the registry itself was authored complete and already
green), then promote the whole-tree `commands/` audit to fail-closed only once the backfill
that makes it green has actually landed (plan 267.3-08, this final wave). Both gates were
proven against a stripped-fixture A/B test before being declared done: a `commands/` copy
with one declaration stripped exits 1, the real tree exits 0.

A fourth option (option-d: narrow the backfill to only the 17 commands blocking Phase 271,
defer the other 50) was argued and explicitly rejected, on the record, for reproducing the
exact debt-ratchet failure just diagnosed: it would leave 50 commands undeclared behind a
gate that only fires when someone happens to stage them, guaranteeing the identical stall at
a time chosen by whatever unrelated phase next collides with it.

## The no-relaxation audit

Every commit across all eight plans that touched a gate or hook file
(`scripts/hooks/`, `scripts/verify-release`, `scripts/check-plugin-path-anchoring.cjs`,
`lib/core/mva-rule-linter.cjs`) was walked and individually verdicted at phase close. Five
commits touched these files across the whole phase (`1967e28d`, `9a1b9fda`, `4b35f2c1`,
`ef1704d4`, `8394073a`); every one is a pure addition -- a new gate, a new vocabulary member,
a new registry mode, or a doc-comment expansion. The one line-level deletion found (in
`ef1704d4`, four lines of a doc comment) was a rewrap of an existing sentence into a longer
comment block, not a removal of any check, predicate, or allowlist entry -- confirmed by
reading the actual diff context, not inferred from the commit message alone. Zero gates were
relaxed, allowlisted, edited to soften a check, or bypassed anywhere in this phase. This is
GUARD-10, and per the ruling it was not negotiable.

## What this phase deliberately did not absorb

Two things named explicitly so a later reader does not assume otherwise. `DEFERRED-271-D2`
(`agents/larry-extended.md` declaring `hitl_shape: F.1` and `connector.excluded: true`
simultaneously, plus 52 `skills/*/SKILL.md` advisory violations) is a Canon Part 11
SHAPE-declaration question on a different field (`hitl_shape` versus `connector.excluded`),
not a reward-declaration question, and stays OPEN with no owner assigned by this phase.
Phase 267.2 still owns repairing the FIRST_INSTALL Reward and Investment legs of the
onboarding flow itself (GAP R-1, the 2/10 Reward score); this phase declared the enforcement
contract for that flow and did not change it. The `session-start:FIRST_INSTALL`,
`:UPDATE` and `:MODE_ROUTING` declarations in `data/first-reward-surfaces.json` are honest
opt-outs describing what ships today, not endorsements of the flow's current shape.

## What can be learned from this, generalized past this one rule

A gate narrowed to a changed/staged scope for a legitimate reason (here: avoiding a
forced-`COMMIT_NO_VERIFY` habit) can still leave the invariant it exists to protect free to
silently reaccumulate wherever the narrowed gate cannot see. The fix is not "don't narrow the
gate" -- narrowing was the right call for the commit-time cadence. The fix is to wire a
whole-tree audit at the SAME TIME the gate is narrowed, even if that audit only runs at a
slower cadence (a release, a periodic sweep), rather than treating the narrowing as complete
and discovering the accumulated gap later via an unrelated collision. This phase's own gate
10d/10e sequencing demonstrates the corollary: wire the gate before the work that makes it
green is done, but do not turn it fail-closed until it actually IS green, or the gate
produces a bypass habit instead of a real backstop. Filed to
`.planning/debug/knowledge-base.md` as `reward-guard-staged-scope-debt-ratchet` for the next
gate designer in this repo to find before making the same narrowing decision blind.
