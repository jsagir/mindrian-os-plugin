## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Hybrid (Multi-Agent governed debate + JTBD-oriented research/intelligence meta-orchestrator)

**Description:**
Two related surfaces sharing one research-ingestion + graph-close-the-loop spine. `/mos:bono`
(evolved from its shipped Phase-164 form) runs a governed, six-hat research debate with
per-persona web research and disconfirming-evidence discipline, concluding in a Minto/MECE
narrative synthesis version-tracked via a SUPERSEDES chain on re-run. `/mos:intel-pipeline`
(net-new) is a JTBD-oriented meta-orchestrator running calibrate -> decompose -> fan research
-> compute -> consolidate -> synthesize -> write-to-graph against any room. Used by the
navigator (Larry's human) to turn an ad-hoc, manually-stitched multi-command research
workflow into one governed, repeatable pass. "Good" = every claim/opportunity/open_question
node lands `proposed` (never auto-confirmed), every persona's assertions trace to its own
wired sources, re-runs version-chain rather than silently duplicate, and the navigator is
gated (F.1/F.5) at every point real judgment or real spend is committed.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. A claim, opportunity, or conclusion node gets auto-confirmed (bypasses `navigation.confirmNode(byUser)`) -- breaks Canon Part 9's human-only truth-promotion invariant.
2. Persona/hat research content, or any conclusion/claim text, egresses to the Brain -- breaks Canon Part 8 (only generic framework/domain handles may cross that boundary).
3. A bono re-run silently duplicates instead of version-chaining (zero or multiple `SUPERSEDES` edges where exactly one is expected), so the navigator can't tell which conclusion is current.
4. Intel-pipeline's calibrate-then-fan sequence reinforces JTBD state without disclosure -- the JTBD orients the search, the search's findings are never checked against JTBD for drift (see Section 6, Guardrail G-2; verified real, not hypothetical, in the 2026-07-15 causal-loop pass -- 223-SPEC.md Addendum).
5. A persona asserts a claim beyond its own wired `INFORMS` source set -- breaks the heterogeneity/disconfirming-evidence discipline Requirement 1 exists to enforce, silently degrading the debate into six voices agreeing with one lens.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** Structured intelligence analysis + strategy synthesis for venture research (an INTERNAL research-methodology tool, not a consumer-facing regulated vertical). The nearest professional disciplines are intelligence-analysis tradecraft (structured analytic techniques) and Minto-Pyramid management-consulting synthesis, both applied to venture/investment diligence.

**User Population:** The navigator (Larry's human) -- a single venture researcher running their own diligence inside a Data Room. Not a public multi-tenant service. The navigator both operates the pipeline and is the sole consumer of its conclusions, and is the only party permitted to promote a proposed claim to confirmed (Canon Part 9).

**Stakes Level:** High. Not life-safety or regulated-industry Critical, but a wrong governed-debate ruling or a fabricated claim that slipped through could mislead a real investment or venture-direction decision downstream. The stakes come from decision-consequence, not from a compliance regime.

**Output Consequence:** The conclusions, claims, opportunities, and open questions land as `proposed` nodes in the room graph and (via the opportunity bank) become the evidence base a navigator reasons over when deciding where to place attention or capital. Acted on, a mis-weighted conclusion steers real spend; a fabricated-but-plausible claim that gets human-confirmed becomes durable graph truth that later analyses inherit. The design's own answer to this (no auto-confirmation, human-only Part 9 truth-promotion) IS the risk control, not a gap to fill.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

**Dimension: Disconfirming-evidence discipline (ACH)**
- Good: the ruling weighs evidence that would FALSIFY the leading hypothesis before evidence that confirms it; the surviving conclusion is the one with the LEAST disconfirming evidence, and the analyst can state what would have killed it. Black hat steelmans then attacks; the leading view was genuinely stress-tested.
- Bad: the conclusion is reached by accumulating confirming evidence (winner-by-confirmation); no disconfirming evidence was sought, or the leading hypothesis was never tested against what would refute it.
- Stakes: Critical
- Source: Heuer/Pherson Analysis of Competing Hypotheses -- "survivor-by-disconfirmation"; a single strong piece of disconfirming evidence eliminates a hypothesis, which is the core anti-confirmation-bias move of intelligence tradecraft.

**Dimension: Heterogeneity of perspective (no premature convergence)**
- Good: the six hats reach the ruling through genuinely different lenses; dissent is preserved and the losing side is steelmanned before Blue rules; no two personas collapse onto one lens.
- Bad: the personas converge early, mirror each other's language or confidence, or agree before thorough scrutiny -- six voices restating one view (sycophantic consensus). The "debate" is theatre.
- Stakes: Critical
- Source: multi-agent-debate research on sycophancy and premature convergence (arXiv 2509.23055, 2509.05396) -- the empirically-verified degradation mode of LLM debate -- plus De Bono parallel-thinking discipline.

**Dimension: Claim provenance (no assertion beyond wired sources)**
- Good: every persona assertion traces to its own wired `INFORMS` source set; a reader can follow any claim to the specific source that grounds it; the conclusion cites no principle that no source in the run supports.
- Bad: a persona asserts beyond its wired sources, or a claim floats with no traceable source; the synthesis states a "well-known" fact that was never actually researched in this run.
- Stakes: Critical
- Source: intelligence-analyst sourcing tradecraft (cite-or-retract) + Canon Part 9 (only sourced, human-ratified claims become truth); this is the fabrication vector that could mislead a venture decision.

**Dimension: Key-Assumptions-Check completed (and JTBD drift surfaced)**
- Good: the load-bearing assumptions behind the leading hypothesis are named and tested FIRST; a false assumption surfaces as an `open_question` rather than being silently baked into the conclusion; when findings diverge from the JTBD that oriented the search, the divergence is disclosed to the navigator.
- Bad: the conclusion rests on unexamined assumptions; no assumptions are registered; the JTBD that oriented the fan is never re-checked against what the research actually found, so the pipeline confirms what it set out to find.
- Stakes: High
- Source: Heuer/Pherson Key Assumptions Check; the 2026-07-15 causal-loop pass (223-SPEC.md Addendum, Guardrail G-2) which verified the JTBD calibrate-then-fan reinforcing loop as real, not hypothetical.

**Dimension: MECE-Minto conclusion structure**
- Good: one governing thought at the top, 3-5 key claims that are mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive (no overlap, no gap), each key claim supported by the evidence beneath it, unknowns declared rather than hidden.
- Bad: overlapping or redundant claims, a gap the claims do not cover, a "conclusion" that is a flat list rather than a pyramid, or a governing thought the key claims do not actually support.
- Stakes: High
- Source: Minto Pyramid Principle (MECE grouping under a single governing thought); the shipped `lib/memory/narrative-schema.cjs` enforces the machine floor (governing_thought <=250 chars, key_claims length 3-5) but MECE-ness is the human-judged quality on top of the schema.

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **Confirmation-driven conclusion (winner-by-confirmation).** The analysis reaches a verdict by piling up supporting evidence and never seeking what would falsify the leading hypothesis. Here it manifests as Black hat failing to argue disconfirming-evidence-first (Failure Mode 5 / Req 1). It looks confident and well-cited, which is exactly why it is dangerous.
2. **Sycophantic premature convergence.** The six personas collapse onto one lens -- mirroring language, matching confidence, agreeing before scrutiny. The double-fan becomes six echoes of one view, and the heterogeneity mandate silently fails. This is the empirically dominant LLM-debate failure, not a hypothetical.
3. **Claim beyond evidence / auto-confirmed fabrication.** A persona asserts past its wired sources, or a plausible-but-unsourced claim is written as a `proposed` node and -- the single most severe breach -- gets promoted to `confirmed` without a human (bypassing `confirmNode(byUser)`). A fabricated claim that becomes durable graph truth misleads every downstream analysis that inherits it (Failure Mode 1, Canon Part 9).
4. **JTBD orientation lock-in.** The fan is oriented by a JTBD that is never re-checked against findings, so the pipeline reinforces its own premise (Failure Mode 4). A reinforcing loop with no counter-signal, verified real in the 2026-07-15 pass.
5. **False version chain / silent duplicate.** A bono re-run that does not version-cut (zero or multiple `SUPERSEDES` edges where exactly one is expected) leaves the navigator unable to tell which conclusion is current -- an analytic record-keeping failure that quietly corrupts the trail of "what we currently believe" (Failure Mode 3, Req 2).

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

No external regulatory regime applies (single-navigator internal venture-research tool, no third-party data subjects, no regulated advice output). The binding constraints are INTERNAL constitutional ones that function as this domain's compliance regime, and they are the design's own risk controls rather than gaps:
- **Canon Part 9 (human-only truth-promotion).** No claim, opportunity, or conclusion may be auto-confirmed; only a human `confirmNode(byUser)` promotes `proposed -> confirmed`. A pipeline that confirms its own output is the most severe breach this phase can commit.
- **Canon Part 8 (graph boundary).** No persona/claim/conclusion content may egress to the Brain; only generic framework/domain handles cross the wire, guarded by `part8-egress-guard.classify`.
These are checked structurally (Part 8 egress test, Part 9 `byUser` fixture in Req 5) rather than by an external auditor.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** In-house extension of `lib/core/bono/*` (Claude-native orchestration on the shipped connector spine)

**Version:** `@modelcontextprotocol/sdk` ^1.29.0, `zod` ^3.25.76, CJS, Node >=22.5.0 (all already-pinned versions from `package.json` - zero new entries)

**Rationale:**
90% of the target architecture already ships and is verified on disk: all 11 reuse-map engines
(`runCellFanout`, `runDebate`, `graph-derivation.runDerivation`, `findings-wirer.wireAccept/wireReject`,
`expert-library.assembleTeam`, `research-context-extractor.extractContext`, `source-lens-driver.runSourceLens`,
`dispatch-optimizer.planDispatch`, `navigation.cjs` + `navigation/edges.cjs`, `narrative-schema.cjs`) exist
and are exercised by Phase 164's shipped `commands/bono.md` (COMPLETE, `run-all-164.sh` 20/20 green). This
phase's net-new work is exactly 4 small files (`hat-governance.cjs`, `persona-research.cjs`,
`commands/intel-pipeline.md`, `skills/intel-pipeline/SKILL.md`) extending that in-house spine - not a
system built from scratch. Every external framework in the matrix is eliminated three times over: (1) it
violates the zero-new-npm-dependency hard constraint (223-SPEC.md Constraints); (2) it introduces a second
egress path Canon Part 8's graph boundary forbids; (3) it duplicates the "one governed reach path / no
second selection brain" connector-spine rule this codebase already enforces structurally
(`dispatchSensors -> decide() -> resolver`).

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| Claude Agent SDK (Anthropic) | Only external option aligned on provider (Claude) + MCP-heavy + safety-first without a model swap - but still a net-new dependency that duplicates the connector spine this plugin already provides while running *inside* that same Agent runtime; circular. |
| LangChain / LlamaIndex / CrewAI / LangGraph / AutoGen / Haystack | All net-new npm dependencies (hard constraint violation); none exist anywhere in this repo's dependency manifest today; each imports its own orchestration/agent-loop brain that would compete with, not extend, the shipped connector spine. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** No - the in-house extension has zero framework lock-in beyond what's already shipped (MCP SDK is a protocol standard, not a vendor-specific agent framework); this was a factor favoring the selection, not a trade-off accepted despite it.

### Entry Point Pattern

The two net-new modules do NOT roll their own agent loop. They are thin adapters that hand a
governance map / a per-persona source set INTO the already-shipped `runCellFanout` and
`runDebate` seams (both expose injectable `dispatchCell` / `deriveFn` / `onStep` hooks
exactly for this). A minimal, copy-runnable sketch of how `hat-governance.cjs` and
`persona-research.cjs` wire into the shipped engines:

```js
'use strict';
// lib/core/bono/hat-governance.cjs -- a PURE map, no I/O. Consumed by runDebate.
const GOVERNANCE = Object.freeze({
  white: { discipline: 'sourced-only, evidence-tiered, cite-or-retract', disconfirming_first: false },
  black: { discipline: 'ACH: disconfirming evidence first; steelman then attack', disconfirming_first: true },
  yellow:{ discipline: 'benefits, evidence-backed never hope',              disconfirming_first: false },
  green: { discipline: 'provocations + alternatives; feed hypotheses to Black', disconfirming_first: false },
  red:   { discipline: 'gut read, timeboxed, no justification',             disconfirming_first: false },
  blue:  { discipline: 'MECE + anti-premature-convergence; strongest model, bias-guarded', judge: true },
});
const CROSS_CUTTING = Object.freeze({
  heterogeneity: true,        // no two persona cells may share an identical lens (Req 1 acceptance)
  anti_convergence: true,     // force dissent + steelman-the-losing-side before ruling
  key_assumptions_check_first: true,
});
module.exports = { GOVERNANCE, CROSS_CUTTING };
```

```js
// how bono's evolved flow composes them (the real call order, engines already shipped):
const fan = await runCellFanout({                 // Fan #1
  subdomains, hats: Object.keys(GOVERNANCE),
  roomDir,                                          // enables planDispatch cost annotation
  dispatchCell: personaResearch.makeDispatchCell(GOVERNANCE), // per-persona extractContext -> runSourceLens -> wireAccept
});
const debate = runDebate({                         // Fan #2
  cells: fan.cells, hypothesis, hypothesisId, hats: Object.keys(GOVERNANCE),
  roomDir, db, expertsByHat,                        // Blue judge = strongest model per GOVERNANCE.blue
  // runDerivationFn / wireAcceptFn default to the SHIPPED graph-derivation + findings-wirer
});
// conclusion: validate the Minto/MECE narrative, then write through the ONE chokepoint
const narrative = { governing_thought, key_claims }; // <=250 / 3-5 items, per SCHEMA_BOUNDS
if (!narrativeSchema.validateNarrative(narrative).ok) throw new Error('narrative invalid');
const concl = navigation.writeGoverningThoughtNode(db, { text: governing_thought, sessionId });
// re-run version cut: link the new conclusion to the prior with exactly one SUPERSEDES edge
if (priorConclusionId) navigation.writeEdge(db, { source: concl.id, target: priorConclusionId, edge_type: 'SUPERSEDES' });
```

### Key Abstractions

| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| `runCellFanout(opts)` (async) | Fan #1. Builds the `(subdomain x hat)` grid, clamps it to the cost cap, dispatches all cells in `Promise.all`, self-critiques and drops low-quality readings. Returns `{cells, dropped, plan, dispatchPlan}`. | The research fan. Inject `dispatchCell` to give each persona its own `runSourceLens` leg (that injection IS `persona-research.cjs`). |
| `runDebate(opts)` | Fan #2. Builds the step sequence per hat, runs consolidators (a confirmed SyntheticExpert becomes the step target), halts at the two Part 3 gates, derives claims via `runDerivation`, wires edges via `wireAccept/wireReject`. | After Fan #1. Feed it `fan.cells` + the governance map so Black argues disconfirming-first and Blue judges on the strongest model. |
| `navigation.cjs` (the chokepoint) | The SINGLE SQL navigation surface. `writeClaimNode` / `writeOpportunityNode` / `writeGoverningThoughtNode` / `writeEdge` / `confirmNode`. Every typed node + `memory_event` is written ONLY through here. | Every graph write in both surfaces. Never call `db.prepare()` to write a node directly -- that bypasses the spine. |
| `ALLOWED_EDGE_TYPES` (frozen Set) | The closed edge allow-list in `navigation/edges.cjs`: `INFORMS`, `REJECTED_BECAUSE`, `CONTRADICTS`, `SUPERSEDES`, `SUPPORTS`, `CONVERGES`, ... | Every `writeEdge` call. If the type you want is not in the Set, you do NOT mint it -- you reconsider the write. `SUPERSEDES` (Req 2) and `INFORMS` (persona sources) are both already present. |
| `planDispatch(roomDir, opts)` | The cost governor. Reads weak sections, selects a model by budget (`selectModel`), scales the swarm to `effectiveBudget = min(remainingContext, maxBudget)`, returns `{agents, model, cost, budget}`. | Sizing the intel-pipeline fan and annotating the bono grid. This is the navigator-facing cost control behind the F.1 fan-approve gate, not a hidden limit. |
| `confirmNode(db, id, byUser, reason)` | The ONLY truth-promotion path (Canon Part 9). Requires an explicit `byUser` arg; promotes `proposed -> confirmed`. | Never call it from a pipeline. The engines write `proposed`; only a human invocation supplies `byUser`. A pipeline that calls it is a Part 9 breach (Failure Mode 1). |

### Common Pitfalls

These are gotchas specific to THIS codebase's conventions, not generic AI-framework advice:

1. **Writing a node with `db.prepare(...).run()` instead of through `navigation.cjs`.** The
   connector spine has one governed reach path (`dispatchSensors -> decide() -> resolver`) and
   one write chokepoint. A direct SQL write skips the typed-edge discipline and the
   `memory_event` audit row, and silently breaks Canon Part 9. Always go through
   `navigation.writeClaimNode` / `writeEdge` / etc.
2. **Auto-confirming a claim.** Every finding, persona claim, conclusion, opportunity, and
   open_question lands `review_status: proposed`. Only `confirmNode(db, id, byUser, ...)` with a
   real human `byUser` promotes it (Failure Mode 1). A pipeline calling `confirmNode` itself, or
   writing a node with `review_status: 'confirmed'`, is the single most severe breach this phase
   can commit.
3. **Minting a new edge type.** `ALLOWED_EDGE_TYPES` is a frozen Set. If your write wants an
   edge that is not in it, the answer is never "add it inline" -- the vocabulary extends only
   additively through `edges.cjs` with a canon rationale (the way `CONTRADICTS`/`SUPERSEDES`
   were added). Use the existing `SUPERSEDES` for the re-run version cut, `INFORMS` for
   persona-to-source, `SUPPORTS`/`CONTRADICTS`/`CONVERGES` for relations.
4. **Letting persona/claim/conclusion content reach the Brain.** Per-persona web research is
   `SIGNAL (public web) -> LOCAL`, never `LOCAL -> BRAIN` (Canon Part 8, Failure Mode 2). Every
   Brain call must carry only generic framework/domain handles, and must pass
   `egressGuard.classify(payload)` returning a non-`block` verdict. `cell-fanout.cjs` already
   uses `genericDomainHandle(subdomain)` for exactly this -- follow that idiom; never pass the
   raw sub-domain text or a persona's finding across the wire.
5. **CJS-only, `process.argv` switch-case, no TypeScript / no Commander.** Both net-new modules
   ship as inspectable `.cjs` source (every output is an edit surface). Do not reach for
   `import`, a `.ts` file, or a CLI arg parser -- the repo convention is a `switch-case` router
   (the `gsd-tools.cjs` pattern) and relative `require()`.
6. **Trusting the hand-written `connector:` block.** The `connector:` tuple on both surfaces
   MUST be regenerated by `node scripts/build-connector-registry.cjs` and verified with
   `--check`; a hand-authored block is a draft, never the final born-wired truth (Req 5,
   223-SPEC.md Risks). `bono` keeps `reach_id: hats` (a `sub_mode` split, not a 7th reach);
   `intel-pipeline` uses the existing `reach_id: context_block`.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model Configuration:**

There is no separate model-provider config to write. Both surfaces run INSIDE Claude Code, so
the top-level reasoning model is whatever Claude Code is currently running as -- this phase does
not open an API client, set a temperature, or hold an API key. The ONE place a model is
selected is the sub-agent fan: `dispatch-optimizer.selectModel` (called by `planDispatch`) picks
a model by budget for the research passes the intel-pipeline dispatches (`Agent` tool,
`run_in_background: true`). Its knobs are budget-driven, not hand-tuned:
- `effectiveBudget = min(remainingContext, maxBudget)`, default `remainingContext = 200000`.
- `DOWNGRADE_THRESHOLD` / `BUDGET_SAFETY_MARGIN` (exported constants) decide when the fan
  downgrades to a cheaper model under budget pressure.
- Blue judge (the debate ruling step) runs on the strongest available model per
  `hat-governance.GOVERNANCE.blue` (Req 1 cross-cutting rule "Blue judge on the strongest
  model"); the six research hats do NOT need the strongest model.
- No temperature is set anywhere -- the debate discipline (heterogeneity, disconfirming-first)
  comes from the governance map and the step sequence, not from a sampling parameter.

**Core Pattern:**

The multi-agent governed-debate + JTBD pipeline of 223-SPEC.md Requirements 1-4 is a
**double-fan-then-close-the-loop**, not an agent-loop-until-done:

1. **Orient (intel-pipeline only).** Read `STATE.md`/`MINTO` + `jtbdState.getCurrent(roomDir)`;
   halt at the F.1 calibrate gate; `jtbdState.setCurrent(roomDir, {jtbd})`; derive research
   dimensions from the JTBD cues. (The 2026-07-15 Addendum flags this as a reinforcing loop --
   `setCurrent` is called once and never re-checked against findings; Guardrail G-2 in Section 6
   is the counter-signal, surfaced at the F.5 synthesize gate.)
2. **Size the fan.** `planDispatch(roomDir, {remainingContext})` returns `{agents, model, cost}`;
   halt at the F.1 fan-approve gate so the navigator sees the cost before spend.
3. **Fan #1 (research).** `runCellFanout` over `(subdomain x hat)` for bono, or N dimension
   passes for intel-pipeline. Each cell/pass runs `extractContext -> runSourceLens -> wireAccept`
   (an `EvidenceClaim proposed` + `INFORMS` edge). A `quality: low` reading HALTs (Req 3). A
   persona may not assert beyond its own wired `INFORMS` set (Req 1, Failure Mode 5).
4. **Fan #2 (governed debate, bono).** `runDebate` consumes the governance map: Black argues
   disconfirming-evidence-first, heterogeneity is asserted (no two cells share a lens),
   anti-premature-convergence forces steelman-the-losing-side before Blue rules.
5. **Synthesize.** Build the Minto/MECE narrative (`governing_thought` <=250 chars, 3-5
   `key_claims`), validate with `narrativeSchema.validateNarrative`; intel-pipeline halts at the
   F.5 synthesize gate (bull/bear ruling + ACH skeptics).
6. **Close the loop.** Write every output through `navigation.cjs` as `proposed` nodes +
   allow-listed edges (contract table below); run `bash scripts/compute-opportunity-state
   <roomDir>`. On a bono re-run, link the new conclusion to the prior with exactly one
   `SUPERSEDES` edge (Req 2; zero on a first run).

**Tool Use:**

The tools are the shipped MCP tools + the Claude Code native `Agent`/web tools, NOT a
framework's tool-decorator registry:
- **In-house engines** (called as plain CJS `require()`, see Section 3 imports) are the bulk of
  the work -- they are not "tools" in the LLM sense, they are the substrate.
- **MCP tools** already registered under `lib/mcp/tools/` (`graph.cjs`, `gate.cjs`, `room.cjs`,
  `sensors.cjs`, `status.cjs`, `chain.cjs`, `stop-gate.cjs`, `views.cjs`) -- the pipeline reaches
  the graph and gates through these on the Desktop/Cowork surfaces; verify the exact tool names
  against `data/connector-registry.json` at wiring time, do not invent new ones.
- **Native Claude Code tools** for the research legs: the `Agent` tool with
  `run_in_background: true` fans the N passes (223-BUILD-BRIEF.md SP-B step 3); web search feeds
  `runSourceLens`. These are Claude Code capabilities, not something this phase installs.
- **Part 8 guard on every Brain touch.** Any call that could reach the Brain passes
  `egressGuard.classify(payload)` first; only a non-`block` verdict (generic handles only)
  proceeds. Persona research never routes to the Brain.

**State Management:**

`room.db` (SQLite, per-room) via `navigation.cjs` is the ONLY persistence path (Canon Part 9;
`feedback_local_graph_sqlite`). Concretely:
- **Nodes/edges:** `navigation.writeClaimNode` / `writeOpportunityNode` /
  `writeGoverningThoughtNode` / `writeEdge`, all landing `review_status: proposed`.
- **Truth promotion:** `navigation.confirmNode(db, id, byUser, reason)` only -- human-supplied
  `byUser`, never a pipeline (Part 9 role 5).
- **JTBD orientation state:** `lib/hmi/jtbd-state.cjs` (its own store), read/written via
  `getCurrent`/`setCurrent`.
- **Chain resumability:** `runDebate` journals each completed step through `pipelineState`
  (`initChain`/`recordStep`) BEFORE the next step, so a crashed run resumes without re-firing.
- **No filesystem state for graph truth:** the room's markdown (`ROOM.md`, `STATE.md`,
  `opportunity-bank/*.md`) is Layer-0 identity, not the graph. Note the 2026-07-15 Addendum
  BLOCKER (Guardrail G-3): `compute-opportunity-state` reads `opportunity-bank/*.md` frontmatter,
  which is a DIFFERENT store from the `room.db` opportunity nodes `writeOpportunityNode` writes.
  Req 4's acceptance needs a bridge (Addendum option a/b/c) before it can pass; do not assume the
  bank rollup sees graph-written opportunities without one.

**Context Window Strategy:**

A six-persona fan with per-hat web research is the budget risk. The cap is structural, not a
prompt trick:
- `runCellFanout` clamps the grid to `resolveFanoutCap` (mirrors `FUTURES_FANOUT_CAP`, threat
  T-164-16 unbounded fan-out); a navigator may shrink the cap, never exceed it.
- `planDispatch` sizes the swarm to `effectiveBudget = min(remainingContext, maxBudget)` and
  downgrades the model under `DOWNGRADE_THRESHOLD` before it ever overflows.
- Each research pass is a SEPARATE background `Agent` with its own fresh window -- the parent
  reasoning context only ingests the `FRAMEWORK_RUNNER_RESULT` summaries, not each pass's raw
  transcript. This is the compaction: fan out to fresh windows, fold back only structured results.
- A `quality: low` reading is dropped (`cell-fanout` self-critique) so low-signal tokens never
  reach the debate step. The F.1 fan-approve and F.5 synthesize gates are where the navigator
  sees and caps real spend (223-SPEC.md Constraints: "the fan-approve / hypothesis-confirm gates
  are the navigator-facing cost controls, not a hidden limit").

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

> Written by `gsd-ai-researcher`. Cross-cutting patterns every developer building AI systems needs -- independent of framework choice.

### Structured Outputs with zod (not Pydantic)

This is a CJS/Node repo -- the structured-output discipline uses `zod` ^3.25.76, not Pydantic.
The existing pattern to follow is `lib/core/behavioral/observation-schema.cjs`: a `.strict()`
zod object that rejects unknown keys BY CONSTRUCTION, plus an explicit forbidden-key guard so a
rejection names the offending field. Apply the identical shape to this phase's two structured
outputs -- the persona claim and the debate conclusion:

```js
// lib/core/bono/persona-research.cjs  -- the structured persona-claim output
const { z } = require('zod'); // in-repo idiom (observation-schema.cjs:19)

// A persona may not assert beyond its own wired sources (Req 1, Failure Mode 5). The schema
// makes that STRUCTURAL: source_ids is required and every claim must name the INFORMS set it
// rode. .strict() rejects any hallucinated extra key (e.g. a smuggled review_status:'confirmed').
const PersonaClaimSchema = z.object({
  hat:        z.enum(['white', 'black', 'yellow', 'green', 'red', 'blue']),
  subdomain:  z.string().min(1),
  text:       z.string().min(1).max(600),
  source_ids: z.array(z.string()).min(1),        // the persona's own wired INFORMS set -- never empty
  disconfirming_first: z.boolean(),              // MUST be true for hat === 'black' (Req 1)
  quality:    z.enum(['high', 'medium', 'low']), // 'low' HALTs the fan (Req 3)
}).strict();

// the conclusion reuses the SHIPPED validator rather than a second schema:
// narrativeSchema.validateNarrative({ governing_thought, key_claims })
//   -- governing_thought <=250 chars, key_claims length in [3,5], each <=200 (SCHEMA_BOUNDS)
module.exports = { PersonaClaimSchema };
```

Integration + retry logic:
- **Where it runs:** validate the persona claim at the `wireAccept` boundary (before the
  `EvidenceClaim proposed` node is written) and validate the conclusion with the shipped
  `narrativeSchema.validateNarrative` before `writeGoverningThoughtNode`. A failed validation
  never writes to `room.db`.
- **Retry:** on a `ZodError`, re-prompt the persona/synthesis step once with the parse error fed
  back (the offending key or the bound it violated), then a second retry; on a third failure,
  DROP the cell (`cell-fanout` already has a self-critique drop path -- record the reason as
  graph data, do not crash the fan). Log every drop with `{hat, subdomain, reason}`. Surface to
  the navigator only if drops exceed the heterogeneity floor (a debate that lost too many
  personas is a Req 1 failure worth a gate, not a silent pass).
- **Never** loosen `.strict()` to `.passthrough()` to make a stubborn model validate -- an extra
  key is usually the model trying to smuggle a composed field (the `observation-schema.cjs`
  lesson: the model emits observations, the engine composes decisions).

### Async-First Design

- **What is actually async here:** `runCellFanout` and `runSourceLens` are `async`;
  `runCellFanout` dispatches every cell with `Promise.all` (parallel), NOT a sequential
  `await`-loop -- that parallelism is the whole point of the fan and must be preserved. `runDebate`
  is intentionally synchronous over its step sequence (the steps are ordered and journaled;
  do not "optimize" it into parallel steps).
- **The one common mistake in THIS runtime:** these modules run inside Claude Code's own event
  loop. Do NOT wrap an engine call in `asyncio.run`-equivalent `(async () => {})()` fire-and-
  forget without awaiting, and do not add a second top-level loop -- a cell error already
  collapses to a defensive stub inside its own promise so one rejection never rejects the whole
  `Promise.all`. Preserve that: keep per-cell `try/catch` returning a stub, never let a single
  research failure reject the fan.
- **Stream vs. await:** stream the persona/debate reasoning to the navigator for UX (they watch
  the hats argue), but AWAIT the full structured output before validation -- you cannot zod-parse
  a half-streamed claim. The pattern is: stream the human-facing narration, await-then-validate
  the machine-facing `PersonaClaimSchema` / narrative JSON before any `room.db` write.
- **Background fan:** intel-pipeline's N research passes use the `Agent` tool with
  `run_in_background: true` and collect `FRAMEWORK_RUNNER_RESULT`; that is the repo's real
  fan-out-to-fresh-windows idiom (223-BUILD-BRIEF.md SP-B step 3), not an in-process thread pool.

### Prompt Engineering Discipline

- **System vs. user separation via the governance map, not free-text.** The per-hat SCRUTINY
  discipline lives in `hat-governance.GOVERNANCE` (the system-role contract: White is
  cite-or-retract, Black is disconfirming-first, Blue is MECE + anti-convergence). The user-role
  content is the sub-domain + JTBD cue only. Keeping the discipline in a frozen map -- not
  re-written per invocation -- is what makes the heterogeneity mandate testable (223-BUILD-BRIEF.md
  Section 5). Do not inline the scrutiny rules into an ad-hoc prompt string per cell.
- **Few-shot: dynamic retrieval, not inline.** A persona's few-shot grounding is its wired
  `INFORMS` source set from `runSourceLens`, retrieved per cell -- not hard-coded exemplars.
  This is deliberate: inline exemplars would collapse heterogeneity (every persona seeing the
  same examples converges on one lens, Failure Mode 5). Each persona reasons only over the
  sources it actually wired.
- **Bound the output explicitly.** `governing_thought` <=250, `key_claims` 3-5 items each <=200
  (`narrative-schema.SCHEMA_BOUNDS`); persona `text` <=600 (schema above). These bounds are
  enforced by validators, not hope -- the analogue of "always set `max_tokens`". Never leave a
  synthesis step to emit an unbounded conclusion; the Minto discipline REQUIRES the ceiling.
- **Voice contract:** no emoji, no em-dashes, 12-glyph vocabulary, 3-line errors, `voice-dna.md`
  honored (223-SPEC.md Acceptance) -- these are prompt-level constraints on every Larry-facing
  string this phase emits, checked by the repo's existing lints.

### Context Window Management

See Section 4 "Context Window Strategy" for the full treatment (fan cap via `resolveFanoutCap`,
budget sizing via `planDispatch`, background `Agent` windows, `quality: low` drop, the F.1/F.5
cost gates). The one-line summary for this system type: **fan out to fresh sub-agent windows,
fold back only validated structured results -- never the raw transcripts** -- and let the two HITL
gates be the human-visible spend ceiling rather than a hidden token cap.

### Cost and Latency Budget

- **Per-run cost driver:** the research fan. A full bono run is up to `(subdomains x 6 hats)`
  cells clamped by `resolveFanoutCap`; intel-pipeline is N dimension passes from `planDispatch`.
  `planDispatch` returns `cost: {perAgent, total, display}` computed from `TOKEN_COSTS` x the
  selected model -- surface `cost.display` AT the F.1 fan-approve gate so the navigator approves
  real spend before it happens (the gate IS the budget control, 223-SPEC.md Constraints).
- **Cheaper models for sub-tasks:** `selectModel` already routes by budget -- the six research
  hats and the routing/classification legs run on a cheaper model; only the Blue judge (the final
  ruling) needs the strongest model (`GOVERNANCE.blue`). Do not run the whole fan on the top model.
- **Caching:** two layers already available in the substrate -- (1) exact-match: `runSourceLens`
  research is filed as `EvidenceClaim` nodes in `room.db`, so a re-run reads wired sources instead
  of re-fetching the web; (2) semantic: the room corpus (`queryRoomCorpus` / research-filing) is
  the reuse path a re-run hits before dispatching a fresh pass. A bono re-run on the same topic
  should reuse the prior run's wired sources and only re-research the deltas, then version-cut with
  `SUPERSEDES` -- not pay for the full fan twice.
- **Latency:** the fan is parallel (`Promise.all`) so wall-clock is roughly one slowest pass, not
  the sum; the two HITL gates (calibrate/fan-approve, synthesize) are the real latency floor and
  they are intentional -- this is a governed judgment pass, not a low-latency lookup.

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

> These dimensions are the MEASURABLE form of Section 1b's five domain rubric ingredients, the
> five Critical Failure Modes (Section 1), the four causal-loop Guardrails (Section 6, G-1..G-4),
> and 223-SPEC.md's per-Requirement Acceptance criteria. They do not re-derive domain context or
> duplicate the acceptance fixtures; they organize what those fixtures assert into eight scored
> dimensions and add the one judged layer the deterministic fixtures cannot reach (real-vs-theatre
> debate quality). Every dimension names the fixture that carries it.

**Measurement taxonomy for THIS repo (read before the table).** There are three approaches, and
the split matters because this is a CJS-only, zero-new-dep, deterministic-CI codebase:

- **Code** = a `node:assert/strict` assertion inside a `tests/test-223-*.cjs` suite, run by the
  blocking `tests/run-all-223.sh` gate. This is the repo's existing convention (96 `run-all-*.sh`
  aggregators ship). It also covers the structured-verdict pattern already used by
  `tests/test-bono-verdict.cjs` (a `record(check, passed, detail)` accumulator emitting
  `{passed, findings[]}`) and the grep sweeps (em-dash, frozen-vocabulary, Part 8 leak,
  `mindrian-designs`). Deterministic. It is the load-bearing measurement here.
- **Judge** = a Claude Code reasoning pass over a SAVED debate/conclusion transcript, scored 1-5
  against the rubric. The "judge" is NOT a new tool: both surfaces already run inside Claude Code,
  so a second Claude reasoning pass over the transcript is already in the runtime at zero
  dependency (this is the only way to satisfy the zero-new-dep constraint AND still score
  subjective quality). It is deliberately kept OUT of `run-all-223.sh` because it is
  non-deterministic and would make the gate flaky. It runs offline (Section 6 flywheel) and is
  calibrated against navigator labels (target >=0.7 correlation before it is trusted, per
  `ai-evals.md`).
- **Human** = the navigator (the sole Part 9 confirmer) plus the two tradecraft-reviewer roles
  from Section 1b (ACH/red-team reviewer for debate transcripts; Minto reviewer for MECE-ness).
  Used for calibration, edge-case review of `proposed` nodes at the F.1/F.5 gates, and final
  arbitration of MECE-ness and JTBD-drift disclosure.

**Concrete rubric per dimension** (domain language, not generic labels):

**D1 - Disconfirming-evidence discipline (ACH).**
- PASS: the Black cell's argument references disconfirming evidence BEFORE any confirming
  evidence (survivor-by-disconfirmation); the persona-claim carries `disconfirming_first: true`;
  the surviving conclusion is the one with the least disconfirming evidence and the analyst can
  state what would have killed it.
- FAIL: the conclusion is reached by accumulating confirming evidence (winner-by-confirmation);
  Black's `disconfirming_first` is `false`, or its evidence array leads with a confirming source.
- Code leg: assert `disconfirming_first === true` for `hat === 'black'` and that the first
  evidence item in Black's ordered array is tagged disconfirming (Req1 debate fixture).
- Judge leg: is the steelman genuine or is Black knocking down a straw hypothesis? (offline)

**D2 - Heterogeneity / no premature convergence.**
- PASS: the six hats reach the ruling through genuinely different lenses; no two persona cells in
  one run share an identical lens-tag; dissent is preserved and the losing side is steelmanned
  before Blue rules.
- FAIL: the personas converge early, mirror each other's language or confidence, or agree before
  scrutiny (sycophantic consensus); the debate is theatre.
- Code leg: the Req1 heterogeneity fixture asserts distinct lens-tags across the fan (a positive
  fixture) AND an adversarial fixture seeding two same-lens cells must be FLAGGED, not passed
  (a negative fixture). If drops for `quality: low` push the surviving fan below the heterogeneity
  floor, that is a Req1 failure worth a gate, not a silent pass (see Section 4b retry logic).
- Judge leg: real divergence vs restating one view (the empirically dominant LLM-debate failure,
  arXiv 2509.23055 / 2509.05396). Scored 1-5 offline, calibrated against the ACH reviewer.

**D3 - Claim provenance (no assertion beyond wired sources).**
- PASS: every persona assertion traces to its own wired `INFORMS` source set; a reader can follow
  any claim to the source that grounds it; the conclusion cites no principle no source supports.
- FAIL: a persona asserts beyond its wired sources, or a claim floats with no traceable source,
  or the synthesis states a "well-known" fact never researched in this run.
- Code leg: `PersonaClaimSchema` (`.strict()`, `source_ids` required min 1) rejects an empty or
  out-of-set citation at the `wireAccept` boundary before any node is written (Req1 source-set
  fixture: a claim citing a source outside its wired `INFORMS` set is rejected). This is the
  fabrication vector; it is enforced structurally, not judged.

**D4 - Human-only truth-promotion (no auto-confirm).**
- PASS: every finding, persona claim, conclusion, opportunity, and open_question lands
  `review_status: proposed`; the only promotion path is `navigation.confirmNode(db, id, byUser,
  reason)` with a real human `byUser`.
- FAIL: any node is written `review_status: 'confirmed'` by a pipeline, or `confirmNode` is
  invoked without a `byUser` argument. This is the single most severe breach this phase can commit.
- Code leg: Req4 fixture asserts all written nodes are `proposed` in room.db and that `confirmNode`
  requires an explicit `byUser` (both surfaces). A grep-level negative asserts neither net-new
  module calls `confirmNode`.

**D5 - Part 8 egress containment.**
- PASS: only generic framework/domain handles cross the Brain wire; `egressGuard.classify(payload)`
  returns a non-`block` verdict on every Brain call; per-persona research is SIGNAL (public web)
  -> LOCAL, never LOCAL -> BRAIN.
- FAIL: persona, claim, or conclusion content egresses to the Brain; `classify` passes a payload
  carrying raw sub-domain or finding text.
- Code leg: Req5 egress test asserts `part8-egress-guard` BLOCKS a seeded LOCAL-content breach and
  that Brain calls carry only generic handles; the `run-all-223.sh` Part 8 grep sweep backstops it.

**D6 - Version-chain integrity (SUPERSEDES).**
- PASS: a re-run on the same topic writes exactly one `SUPERSEDES` edge from the new conclusion to
  the prior; a first run writes zero; `--version-log` walks the chain in order.
- FAIL: a re-run produces zero or multiple `SUPERSEDES` edges (the navigator cannot tell which
  conclusion is current), or a first run mints a false chain.
- Code leg: the Req2 two-run fixture asserts exactly one edge; the one-run fixture asserts zero.
  This is a correctness bug when it fails, not a quality signal (Section 6 flywheel row 1).

**D7 - Key-Assumptions-Check + JTBD-drift disclosure.**
- PASS: the load-bearing assumptions behind the leading hypothesis are named and tested first; a
  false assumption surfaces as an `open_question` rather than being baked into the conclusion; when
  synthesize-step findings diverge from the JTBD `setCurrent` locked at calibrate time, the
  divergence is surfaced at the F.5 gate for the navigator to decide (Guardrail G-2).
- FAIL: the conclusion rests on unexamined assumptions; no assumptions are registered; the JTBD is
  never re-checked against findings, so the pipeline confirms what it set out to find (FM4, the
  2026-07-15 causal-loop reinforcing loop).
- Code leg: a fixture proves a `--dry-run` (and real) intel-pipeline run does NOT silently update
  JTBD state as a side effect of its findings without a navigator-facing gate (223-SPEC.md
  Addendum, Req3).
- Judge + Human leg: was the divergence disclosed meaningfully; navigator decides re-calibrate.

**D8 - MECE-Minto conclusion structure.**
- PASS: one governing thought (<=250 chars) at the top; 3-5 key claims that are mutually exclusive
  and collectively exhaustive (no overlap, no gap), each supported by the evidence beneath it;
  unknowns declared as `open_question` nodes rather than hidden.
- FAIL: overlapping or redundant claims, a gap the claims do not cover, a flat list rather than a
  pyramid, or a governing thought the key claims do not support.
- Code leg: `narrativeSchema.validateNarrative` enforces the machine floor (governing_thought
  <=250, key_claims length 3-5, each <=200) before `writeGoverningThoughtNode` (Req2).
- Judge + Human leg: MECE-ness above the schema floor is the Minto reviewer's call, scored 1-5.

## 7. Production Monitoring

> "Production" here is not a multi-tenant service. It is the navigator running these two surfaces
> live inside Claude Code against one room (Section 1b user population: a single venture
> researcher). So monitoring is not an APM dashboard; it is the audit trail the shipped spine
> already writes, read by the navigator and by the offline flywheel. No new tool, by the same
> zero-new-dep + Part 8 constraint that governs Section 5.

**Tracing Tool:** The shipped audit substrate, not an external tracer:
- `room.db` `memory_event` rows - `navigation.cjs` journals one audit row for every node/edge
  write, so every `proposed` claim, `SUPERSEDES` edge, and any `confirmNode` call is already
  traced with its `byUser` (or absence). This is the trace of record.
- `pipelineState` chain journal (`initChain` / `recordStep`) - `runDebate` and the intel-pipeline
  fan journal each completed step before the next, so a run's step sequence (and a resume) is
  reconstructable without a second tool.
- The F.1 / F.5 gate transcripts - the human-visible record of what the navigator saw and approved
  at each spend/judgment gate.

**Key Metrics to Track** (all readable from the audit substrate above):
1. **Auto-confirm attempts** (D4) - count of any `confirmNode` without a `byUser`, or any node
   written `review_status: 'confirmed'` by a pipeline session. Target: exactly 0. This is the
   catastrophic metric.
2. **Part 8 egress verdicts** (D5) - count of `egressGuard.classify` calls returning non-`block`
   on any payload carrying LOCAL content. Target: exactly 0.
3. **SUPERSEDES chain integrity** (D6) - per re-run topic, edges != 1 on a re-run or != 0 on a
   first run. Target: exactly 0 violations.
4. **Proposed-claim volume vs. human-confirm rate** (G-4, Loop 4) - ratio of AI-`proposed` claims
   to human-`confirmed` claims per room, over a rolling window. Rising volume with a low confirm
   rate means the pipeline is out-generating the navigator's actual review.
5. **JTBD-drift disclosures** (D7, G-2) - count of synthesize-step divergences surfaced at the F.5
   gate vs. silently filed. Target: every divergence surfaced; 0 silent.

**Alert Thresholds:**
- **PAGE / block the release gate (correctness, not drift):** any auto-confirm attempt > 0 (metric
  1); any Part 8 non-`block` on LOCAL content (metric 2); any re-run with SUPERSEDES != 1
  (metric 3). These are `run-all-223.sh` failures - they fail the gate, they do not "alert."
- **FLAG for navigator review (quality drift, offline):** proposed:confirmed ratio exceeds a wide
  margin with a low confirm rate (metric 4); a heterogeneity collapse or genuine-steelman failure
  flagged by the judge pass (D2/D1); a JTBD divergence that was not surfaced (metric 5). These
  route to the Section 6 offline flywheel, not to a page.

**Smart Sampling Strategy** (weight toward concerning signals, per `ai-evals.md`):
- 100%-sample the cheap structural checks (metrics 1-3, 5) - they are deterministic and already run
  in the gate; there is no cost reason to sample them.
- Weight the EXPENSIVE judge pass (D2 real-vs-theatre, D1 genuine-steelman, D8 MECE-ness) toward
  the transcripts most likely to have degraded: (a) a run where a cell was dropped for
  `quality: low`; (b) a run where drops pushed the surviving fan near the heterogeneity floor;
  (c) a debate that reached its ruling in fewer steps than expected (a convergence-too-fast signal);
  (d) a room whose proposed-claim volume spiked (metric 4). A clean, full-fan, normal-length run is
  sampled at a low baseline rate.
- Watch for signal-metric divergence: if the navigator confirms few `proposed` claims while the
  deterministic gate stays green, that gap (not a red metric) is the early warning that the judged
  quality dimensions are drifting below what the structural checks can see - investigate manually.
