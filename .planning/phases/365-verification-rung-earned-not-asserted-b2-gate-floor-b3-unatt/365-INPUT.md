# Phase 365 planning input: verification rung, earned not asserted

## Why (one paragraph)

The paper author's AI-literacy papers name one failure above all: checking a claim by asking a
model feels like verification and never leaves the loop ("rung 2"). A room of human-approved
claims with provenance edges LOOKS verified, so if those claims were model-checked the
architecture lends rung-2 output the standing of rung 3 or 4. That is worse than a chat window,
because it looks like rigor. The fix is a rung the architecture EARNS from edges, not one the
filer declares. It is also the instrument for the paper author's own experiment (Mindrian vs a
disciplined person with a chat window and a notebook), which cannot run until the room can
answer "which of these claims was never really checked".

## The draft ladder (for the paper author to RATIFY; do not freeze)

- 0 Unchecked. Filed and not tested.
- 1 Navigator recall or intuition.
- 2 Model-internal: asked a model, got agreement. THE LAUNDERING RUNG. "More time inside the loop is not the same as leaving it."
- 3 Secondary document: summary, aggregator, abstract, plain-language version.
- 4 Primary source, LOCATED: the specific part that decides the claim, not just cited.
- 5 A person with standing who disagreed, or who could have and did not.

Structural predicates (so the rung is earned):
- 3+ requires an outbound edge to a node carrying `url` and `retrieved_at` (fields already exist on the research-claim schema).
- 4+ additionally requires a `locator` (page, section, DOI, clause). Lesson: a summary and the authors' conclusions of the same review said different things; the right source is not enough, the right PART is.
- 5 requires an edge to a person node (B1a).
- A declared rung the edges do not support is written at the supported rung, and the discrepancy is recorded, not discarded.
- Extend alongside the four locked provenance fields (`source`, `url`, `retrieved_at`, `evidence_tier`); never rename or drop them.

## Guardrails (already right; do not "improve")

- The single write chokepoint (navigation.cjs / node-insert.cjs).
- Truth claims default to `proposed`; only a human confirms.
- The system-bookkeeping carve-out stays narrow and documented.
- Closed enums fail shut; additive extension only, no coercion, no "other".

## Do NOT build

- A confidence score on claims (gives the calibration gap somewhere comfortable to hide).
- Auto-promotion to confirmed on any signal (edge volume, agent agreement, time, tier).
- A model that grades the rung ("rung 2 grading rung 2, wearing a badge that says 4").

## Acceptance (run FIRST, before code changes, to record the failing baseline)

1. Byte test: two claims with identical text, one checked against a located primary source, one by asking a model. Today identical rows; after: differ in a filterable field.
2. One-week test: approve a model-checked claim; later ask the room what it was checked against. After B1+B2: names the rung and the claim never reached `confirmed`.
3. Floor test: floor at rung 3, approve a rung-2 claim. After B2: lands `needs_evidence`, approver saw why before clicking.

Property falsification tests (from the review draft):
1. Two navigators, same framework, same room: do the frames diverge as far as two unguided prompts?
2. Remove the destination (governing thought + active JTBD): does next-move confidence stay unchanged?
3. The missing five: claims covering 1-4 and 6-7 of a continuous set; predicted to FAIL (gap scans map the record, not the world).
4. Ask the room how it knows (same as acceptance 2).
5. Contradiction without shared wording. NOTE: `findContradictions` only reads existing CONTRADICTS edges; the test really targets the ~8 writers that create them (graph-derivation, findings-wirer, ...). Planning must name which writer(s) the test drives.

Measure afterwards: the rung distribution across an established room. Most confirmed claims at 1-2 is the fix working, not failing.
