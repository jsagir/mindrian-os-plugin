# 369.26 Canon v5 alignment: the terminal mod switches to the website's role colors

> Scope: a spec amendment. It changes no file under `ui/mindrian-workspace-mod/src`, no test, no guard. Everything here is the contract for the NEXT execution quick. Visible words are named by copy deck ID (plain-English hard rule); the only new words are in section 5, in the copy-deck proposal table, and they enter `369.26-UI-SPEC.md` section 9 and `src/copy/deck.ts` together in that quick (the copy guard compares the two, so section 9 is not edited here).

## 0. The decision in one paragraph

The mod stops using blue, yellow, red, black and cream as "where you are / your move / a problem / the frame / the page" (the Mondrian-concept roles) and uses the Canon v5 roles instead. Black is structure and the human gate. Paper (cream) is the human canvas. Blue is evidence. Red is an assumption that needs examination. Yellow is a contradiction. Dashed ink on paper is unknown. Flat fills only. Each color has exactly one role. A machine suggestion never looks like a human decision. The five concept images still fix layout, tiers, words and keys; they no longer fix colors.

What the canon says, with the sections I read (all of `DESIGN-CANON.md`, the four linked design docs, the v5 amendment draft; the website has no `DESIGN.md`):

| Canon source | Rule used here |
|---|---|
| s8 v5 palette (D-V5-02) | paper, ink and five role colors only; black structure and human gate, blue evidence, red assumption needing examination, yellow contradiction (filled plane, ink text); dashed ink on paper = unknown; flat fills only (no gradient, shadow, rounded corner, transparency, circle, Venn overlap); teal retired |
| s8 v4 table | still governs article and doc surfaces: "yellow = human attention", "red = tension, not generic error". The v4 rows that conflict with v5 yellow are NOT used by the mod (OQ-13) |
| s9, s12 | the two squares are anchors, never decoration; questions are visual objects with more weight than body copy |
| s13 | credible conflicting readings stay unresolved; no compromise card |
| s14 | a human gate is a threshold: stronger framing, whitespace, quieter surroundings, clear consequence, explicit options; a recommendation must never look like a human decision already made |
| s15, s34 | machine confidence never impersonates human acceptance; human and machine are distinguished by behavior, not by avatar |
| s31 | square corners, thin ink borders, open-sided containers, partial frames |

## 1. Role-by-role map (every current use of every color, and its v5 role)

Job names in code change with the roles (section 4): `where` -> `evidence`, `yourMove` -> `contradiction`, `problem` -> `assumption`, `frame` -> `structure`, `reading` -> `paper`; `logoGreen` stays. The name IS the five-squares test: a job can only be used for its role.

Treatment vocabulary used below:

| Term | Meaning in the terminal |
|---|---|
| ink plane | a flat black block, cream words (17.13). Bold when it needs a person. |
| doorway | a one-column paper cell inside the left edge of an ink plane. Only a real decision gets it (the human gate threshold). |
| paper plane | a flat cream block, black words (17.13), bold label word. |
| evidence plane | a flat blue block, cream words (9.82). |
| assumption plane | a flat red block, cream words (5.56). |
| unknown | paper with a dashed ink run: the words, then a run of hyphen-space glyphs to the end of the block (band), or an open-sided frame of two dashed rules above and below (pane). Hyphen-space is an ASCII glyph, so it survives NO_COLOR and is not a word. |
| ink chip | a Button on an ink ground (C-29). Every Button is an ink chip unless it sits on an evidence or assumption plane. |

### 1.3 What is NOT drawn (and why)

Yellow is not drawn anywhere in the mod. Canon yellow means a contradiction, and the mod has no source for one. The tool exists: `contradiction_check` in `lib/mcp/tools/sensors.cjs` (a pull, `navigation.findContradictions`, returns node ids, needs a focus node or the session's active focus, otherwise returns an empty list with a note). It is NOT in the mod's audited tool set (`MINDRIAN_TOOLS` in `tests/test-369.26-source-guards.cjs` is `status_read`, `gate_list`, `gate_answer`, `gate_render`, `room_artifact`, `whitespace_scan`), the mod holds no node id to pass, and the result carries ids not titles. So: no source in the mod, no yellow. It is never invented, never used as a warning, never used for the gate. The follow-up that adds a source is in section 4.9, with reserved (not yet added) copy.

## 3. Rules that follow

1. Jobs are role names; a plane is drawn with the job of its role only. The role lock is a guard (G12, section 4.6).
2. Buttons: every Button is an ink chip (C-29). The only other legal grounds: the evidence plane (Sources rows, P72 plane) and the assumption plane (P75 pick rows). No `variant="primary"` anywhere.
3. Selected state: a `>` marker and bold words; the active tab is a non-pressable bold `Text` on paper. No selection color.
4. A suggestion has no visual weight a choice lacks. The recommended option is named only in the suggestion line.
5. Initial focus on the Review tab and on any card never lands on a numbered choice: it goes to the tab strip, so Enter cannot commit a choice by accident. Digits stay armed only with pane focus (R-03 stays).
6. At most one ink GATE plane per view (the decision). Needs-a-person planes in the band may add a second ink plane beside it, separated by a paper cell. In the pane, a needs-a-person plane (refusal) sits inside the card or is the only ink plane.
7. Adjacent planes of different roles are separated by a paper cell or a blank row, never touching (blue next to red is 1.77, blue next to black 1.75). Section 4.8 lists the pairs.
8. No gradients, no round corners (`borderStyle` "round" is banned), no `dimColor` on paper or on colored planes, no transparency, no circle glyphs.
9. Plain mode and NO_COLOR carry the same meaning in words, bold and borders. Nothing in section 1 depends on color alone: every plane has its words, the unknown has its words and its hyphens, the gate has its words and a `!` in plain mode.
