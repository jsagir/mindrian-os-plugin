<objective>
Close finding F-1: the Feynman-MINTO guardian's on-stop invariant report is produced and then thrown away twice over, so a navigator never learns a section breached its reasoning contract.

Two independent defects live in the same shell line (`scripts/on-stop`, the Phase 88-13 guardian block):

1. **The discard.** The invocation ends in `>/dev/null 2>&1 || true`, so the `{"systemMessage": ...}` line `runOnStop` writes to its own stdout never leaves that subprocess. Even with the redirect removed, `scripts/on-stop` builds its FINAL stdout JSON independently from `STOP_SUMMARY_LINE` / `VOICE_SUMMARY_LINE` and never folds the guardian in. Claude Code reads only that final line, so removing the redirect alone fixes nothing. Both halves must change.
2. **The kill.** `timeout 1` sends SIGTERM at 1000ms. `runOnStop` walks every section FIRST, then prunes ghosts, then writes `.mindrian/invariant-report.json`, then emits the systemMessage, all sequentially. A slow validator or a large room therefore kills the process before any of the three outputs exist, and `|| true` swallows the exit-124 evidence that a kill happened.

Purpose: satisfy success criterion 1's BOTH legs. SC1 is compound and this plan treats it as two separately-testable claims, per RESEARCH.md Pitfall 1.

Output: an honest, reachable guardian report on the default CLI Stop path.

**Resolution R-04 recorded here (RESEARCH.md Open Question 3, "raise the timeout vs. restructure runOnStop's program order").** Adopt BOTH, because each alone leaves a real hole. RESEARCH.md's recommendation was to remove the hard kill on the write and prune phase specifically while respecting the outer 3000ms Stop-hook budget as a hard external constraint. Raising the outer ceiling alone still lets one pathological validator eat the whole budget and starve the write. Reordering alone cannot help, because the report content genuinely depends on the completed walk. So: the outer `timeout` ceiling rises from 1s to a 3s safety net (still inside the Stop budget, and now a genuine last-resort ceiling rather than a routine kill), AND `runOnStop` gains its own soft wall-clock deadline that bounds ONLY the section-walk loop. When the soft deadline trips, the walk stops early, the report records exactly how far it got, and the prune plus write plus systemMessage always run. A slow report write is then covered by the raised ceiling; a slow walk is covered by the soft deadline.
</objective>
